# Lesson 1: Greetings & Introductions
### Module 2 — your first real conversation in Portuguese

**Goal:** By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to greet someone, ask how they are, introduce yourself, and say goodbye — naturally, like a Brazilian.

## 1. Essential Greetings & Introductions

Read each line: Portuguese → simple pronunciation → English meaning. Say it out loud — Portuguese is meant to be spoken!

| # | Portuguese | Pronunciation (simple phonetics) | Meaning | Usage note |
|---|-----------|----------------------------------|---------|------------|
| 1 | **Olá** | oh-LAH | Hello | Friendly and universal. A notch warmer than English "hello." |
| 2 | **Oi** | oy | Hi | Very casual, like "hey." Perfect with friends, peers, shopkeepers. |
| 3 | **Bom dia** | bom DEE-uh | Good morning | Used until ~noon. (Note: *bom* is nasal — hum through the "o.") |
| 4 | **Boa tarde** | BOH-uh TAR-jee | Good afternoon | Noon until evening. You'll hear this everywhere. |
| 5 | **Boa noite** | BOH-uh NOY-chee | Good evening / Good night | Both "good evening" (arriving) and "good night" (leaving). Context tells. |
| 6 | **Tudo bem?** | TOO-doo beng? | How's it going? / All good? | *The* Brazilian greeting. More common than "como vai?" — say it with a smile. |
| 7 | **Tudo bem, e você?** | TOO-doo beng, ee voh-SEH? | I'm good, and you? | The natural reply. *Você* = "you" (informal-standard in Brazil). |
| 8 | **Meu nome é...** | meh-oo NOH-mee eh... | My name is... | Fill in your name: *Meu nome é Ana.* |
| 9 | **Prazer em conhecê-lo / conhecê-la** | pruh-ZEHR eng koh-nyeh-SEH-loo / -luh | Nice to meet you | Use **-lo** for a man, **-la** for a woman. Short version: just **Prazer!** |
| 10 | **Obrigado / Obrigada** | oh-bree-GAH-doo / oh-bree-GAH-duh | Thank you | Men say **obrigado**, women say **obrigada**. (Literally "obliged.") |
| 11 | **Por favor** | poor fuh-VOHR | Please | Sprinkle generously — Brazilians notice politeness. |
| 12 | **Com licença** | kong lee-SENG-suh | Excuse me | To get attention, pass by someone, or start a polite request. |
| 13 | **Desculpe / Desculpa** | jish-KOOL-pee / jish-KOOL-puh | Sorry / Excuse me | **Desculpe** is a bit more formal; **desculpa** is casual. Both fine in Brazil. |
| 14 | **Tchau** | chow | Bye | Casual goodbye (from Italian *ciao*). Warm, not cold. |
| 15 | **Até logo / Até mais** | uh-TEH LOH-goo / uh-TEH mighsh | See you later | **Até logo** = "until soon"; **Até mais** = "until more" (very Brazilian, very common). |

### Pronunciation tips (nasal sounds made easy)
- **ão** (in *não*, *coração*, *mão*): say "ow" while letting air hum through your nose. Think of the English word "now" said through a stuffy nose — that's close!
- **ã / õ** (in *manhã*, *coração*): same idea — the vowel is "nasalized," meaning some air escapes through your nose. Put a finger lightly on the side of your nose: you should feel a tiny buzz.
- **nh** (in *conhecê-la*): like the "ny" in "canyon."
- **lh** (in *trabalho*): like the "lli" in "million."
- **R at the start** of a word (in *Rio*): a soft breathy "h" — *HEE-oo*. In the middle of words it's a light tap, like the "tt" in American "butter."

## 2. você vs. tu vs. o senhor / a senhora — who to say "you" to

This confuses learners, but Brazil makes it simple:

- **você** — your default "you" in Brazil. Works with friends, strangers, colleagues, shopkeepers. Use this 95% of the time and you'll be fine.
- **tu** — common in parts of the South and Northeast, but *used with você-style verb forms*. As a learner, you don't need it — just recognize it.
- **o senhor** (to a man) / **a senhora** (to a woman) — respectful "you" for elderly people, authority figures, or very formal settings. Literally "the sir / the lady." Example: *O senhor precisa de ajuda?* (Do you need help, sir?)

**Portugal note:** In Portugal, *tu* is the normal informal "you" and *você* can sound distant — the reverse of Brazil. The course flags these differences as they come up.

## 3. Three Mini-Dialogues

### Dialogue A — Meeting someone at a party
- **Carlos:** Oi! Tudo bem?
- **You:** Tudo bem, e você?
- **Carlos:** Tudo ótimo! Meu nome é Carlos.
- **You:** Prazer, Carlos! Meu nome é [your name].
- **Carlos:** Prazer em conhecê-la! *(or conhecê-lo if you're a man)*

*"Hi! How's it going?" / "I'm good, and you?" / "Great! My name is Carlos." / "Nice to meet you, Carlos! My name is ___." / "Nice to meet you!"*

### Dialogue B — Greeting a colleague at work
- **You:** Bom dia, Dona Marta!
- **Marta:** Bom dia! Como vai?
- **You:** Vou bem, obrigada. E a senhora?
- **Marta:** Bem também, obrigada.

*"Good morning, Mrs. Marta!" / "Good morning! How are you?" / "I'm well, thank you. And you (formal)?" / "Well too, thank you."*

### Dialogue C — Saying goodbye to a new friend
- **You:** Foi um prazer te conhecer!
- **Friend:** Igualmente! Até mais!
- **You:** Até logo! Tchau!

*"It was a pleasure meeting you!" / "Likewise! See you!" / "See you later! Bye!"*

## 5. Cultural Note: The Warmth of Brazilian Greetings 🇧🇷

Brazilians don't just say hello — they *greet*. Expect warmth: eye contact, a genuine smile, and often physical closeness that might surprise you at first.

**The beijinho (little kiss):** Between women, and between men and women who know each other, a kiss on the cheek is standard — **one kiss** in most of Brazil (São Paulo, Rio), **two kisses** in some regions (like Minas Gerais), and **three** in a few places. Between men, it's usually a handshake or a hug. As a visitor, follow the other person's lead — offer your cheek and let them guide you.

**The long goodbye:** Don't be surprised if "goodbye" takes ten minutes. Brazilians say tchau, then keep talking, then say até logo, then talk more. It's affection, not inefficiency.

**Names matter:** Brazilians often go by first names even in professional settings, and nicknames are everywhere. If someone introduces themselves as "Beto," call them Beto — using their full name "Roberto" would sound oddly formal.
