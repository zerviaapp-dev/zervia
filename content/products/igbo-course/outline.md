# Learn Igbo — Full Course Curriculum

**Course:** Learn Igbo: Speak with Confidence, from First Greeting to Real Conversation
**Taught in:** English, for English speakers
**Level:** Complete beginner → conversational
**Approach:** Practical and conversational. Every lesson teaches language you can use the same day — in greetings, at the market, with family — with cultural notes woven in. Tones and pronunciation are coached throughout, because Igbo is a tonal language: the pitch of your voice can change a word's meaning.

**How the course works:** Short lessons, each built around real phrases and mini-dialogues. Each module ends with a guided conversation-practice set so you speak, not just read.

---

## Module 1 — Sounds, Tones & Your First Words
Pronunciation foundations. Igbo's alphabet, special characters (ị, ọ, ụ, ṅ), and the tone system explained simply — high, low, and downstep tones with listening-friendly examples.

- Lesson 1.1: The Igbo alphabet and special letters (ị, ọ, ụ, ṅ, gb, kp, ch)
- Lesson 1.2: Vowel harmony — why it matters for sounding natural
- Lesson 1.3: High tone vs low tone: hearing the difference (with word pairs like ákwá/àkwà)
- Lesson 1.4: Downstep and tone in sentences
- Lesson 1.5: Your first 10 words — saying them out loud with correct tone
- Lesson 1.6: Pronunciation drill set — record-and-compare practice

## Module 2 — Greetings & Introductions
The heart of Igbo social life. Time-of-day greetings, asking names, introducing yourself and others, and the respectful forms used with elders.

- Lesson 2.1: Ndewo! — the everyday hello and when to use it
- Lesson 2.2: Time-of-day greetings — Ụtụtụ ọma, Ehihie ọma, Mgbede ọma
- Lesson 2.3: Kedu? and Kedu ka ị mere? — asking "how are you" like a local
- Lesson 2.4: Aha m bụ… — saying your name and asking others' names
- Lesson 2.5: Biko and Daalụ — please and thank you, used constantly
- Lesson 2.6: Nnọọ — welcoming someone, and responding to a welcome
- Lesson 2.7: Ka ọ dị — saying goodbye warmly
- Lesson 2.8: Conversation practice — your first full introduction dialogue

## Module 3 — Numbers, Time & Dates
Counting, telling time, days, and the Igbo four-day market week (Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ).

- Lesson 3.1: Numbers 1–10 (Otu, Abụọ, Atọ… Iri)
- Lesson 3.2: Numbers 11–100 and the counting pattern
- Lesson 3.3: Money numbers — thousands and millions in everyday speech
- Lesson 3.4: Telling the time and parts of the day
- Lesson 3.5: Days of the week and the four Igbo market days
- Lesson 3.6: Dates, months, and talking about "when"
- Lesson 3.7: Conversation practice — making plans and appointments

## Module 4 — Food & Dining
Ordering food, talking about dishes (fufu/akpu, ofe nsala, abacha, nkwobi), and the etiquette of eating with others.

- Lesson 4.1: Core food vocabulary — staples, soups, and snacks
- Lesson 4.2: "I want / I like" — ordering at a buka or restaurant
- Lesson 4.3: Asking what's in a dish and about spice levels
- Lesson 4.4: Compliments to the cook — phrases that earn smiles
- Lesson 4.5: Eating together — sharing, washing hands, and table manners
- Lesson 4.6: Drinks — palm wine (mmanya nkwụ), malt, and offering a drink
- Lesson 4.7: Conversation practice — a full meal-ordering dialogue

## Module 5 — Shopping & Money at the Market
Bargaining politely, asking prices, quantities, and the market-day rhythm of Igbo commerce.

- Lesson 5.1: Market vocabulary — stalls, sellers, and common goods
- Lesson 5.2: Ego ole? — "How much?" and understanding prices
- Lesson 5.3: Bargaining with grace — polite haggling phrases
- Lesson 5.4: Quantities — "a little," "plenty," weights and measures
- Lesson 5.5: Paying — cash, transfer, and confirming the price
- Lesson 5.6: Sizes, colors, and describing what you want
- Lesson 5.7: Conversation practice — buying yams at the market, start to finish

## Module 6 — Travel & Directions
Getting around: taxis, keke (tricycles), asking the way, and key transport phrases.

- Lesson 6.1: Transport vocabulary — taxi, bus, keke, okada
- Lesson 6.2: "Where is…?" — Ebee ka… dị? and understanding answers
- Lesson 6.3: Direction words — left, right, straight, near, far
- Lesson 6.4: Hiring a ride and agreeing the fare first
- Lesson 6.5: "Stop here" and other in-journey phrases
- Lesson 6.6: Asking for help when you're lost
- Lesson 6.7: Conversation practice — navigating to a compound

## Module 7 — Family & Social Life (Umunna Culture)
Family terms, the extended-family (umunna) system, weddings, naming ceremonies, and social visits.

- Lesson 7.1: Family vocabulary — Nna, Nne, Nwanne, and the extended web
- Lesson 7.2: Umunna explained — why "brother" means more than sibling
- Lesson 7.3: Visiting someone's home — greetings on arrival and departure
- Lesson 7.4: Weddings and traditional marriage — key terms and phrases
- Lesson 7.5: Naming ceremonies and congratulating new parents
- Lesson 7.6: Condolences — what to say in times of loss, respectfully
- Lesson 7.7: Conversation practice — meeting the extended family

## Module 8 — Work & Business Phrases
Professional small talk, meetings, phone calls, and doing business the Igbo way — relationship first.

- Lesson 8.1: Professional introductions and titles at work
- Lesson 8.2: Small talk before business — the warm-up that matters
- Lesson 8.3: Phone call phrases — calling, holding, and callbacks
- Lesson 8.4: Meetings — agreeing, suggesting, and asking questions
- Lesson 8.5: Negotiating and closing a deal politely
- Lesson 8.6: Email and message closings in Igbo
- Lesson 8.7: Conversation practice — a first business meeting

## Module 9 — Emergencies & Health
The phrases you hope you'll never need: asking for help, describing symptoms, pharmacies, and hospitals.

- Lesson 9.1: Nyere m aka! — "Help me!" and getting attention fast
- Lesson 9.2: Describing pain and symptoms simply
- Lesson 9.3: At the pharmacy — asking for medicine
- Lesson 9.4: At the clinic or hospital — key phrases
- Lesson 9.5: Allergies, conditions, and "I need a doctor"
- Lesson 9.6: Emergency numbers and asking someone to call for you
- Lesson 9.7: Conversation practice — explaining a problem at a clinic

## Module 10 — Culture, Etiquette & Conversation Practice
Respect, titles (Mazi, Da, Nna, Nne), proverbs, and bringing everything together in flowing conversation.

- Lesson 10.1: Titles of respect — Mazi, Da, Nna, Nne, and when to use them
- Lesson 10.2: Greeting elders properly — the customs behind the words
- Lesson 10.3: Kola nut (ọjị) culture — the ritual of welcome
- Lesson 10.4: Proverbs (ilu) — a few famous ones and what they teach
- Lesson 10.5: Do's and don'ts — etiquette that keeps you welcome
- Lesson 10.6: Storytelling phrases — "once upon a time" and narrative flow
- Lesson 10.7: Final conversation practice — a full social visit, start to finish
- Lesson 10.8: Your next steps — keeping Igbo alive after the course

---

**Total: 10 modules, 71 lessons.** Estimated study time: 8–12 weeks at 20–30 minutes a day, with lifetime access to revisit anything.
