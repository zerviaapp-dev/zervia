## FAQ

**1. Can I service my own furnace?**
Honest answer: you can do the *maintenance*, not the *servicing* — and the distinction matters. Changing filters, keeping vents clear, checking thermostat batteries, and keeping the area around your furnace clean: absolutely, and this course teaches you how. Anything involving gas lines, burners, heat exchangers, electrical components inside the unit, or refrigerant: that's licensed-technician work, full stop. Opening up a furnace you don't understand can void warranties, create carbon monoxide risks, and in some jurisdictions violate code. This course draws the line clearly in every module — you'll know exactly which side of it you're on.

**2. How often should I change my furnace filter?**
The honest answer: it depends on your filter, your home, and your life. A basic 1-inch filter in a home with pets might need changing every 30 days; a thick 4-inch media filter in a pet-free home can go 6 months. The course gives you a real decision framework (filter thickness + MERV rating + pets + allergies + system runtime = your schedule) plus a printable Filter-Change Schedule you fill in once and follow all year. The one universal rule: a filter you never change is worse than a cheap filter you change on time.

**3. Will this course actually lower my energy bills?**
The course teaches the measures with the best return for the least effort: proper filter maintenance (a clogged filter makes your system work harder for the same heat), thermostat setback strategy (the single biggest no-cost saver), sealing the obvious duct and envelope leaks, and understanding your efficiency ratings so your next equipment decision is an informed one. Realistic expectation: diligent application typically trims heating and cooling costs by a noticeable margin — the course is honest that no single tip is magic, but the stack of small wins is real. In an Alberta winter, even single-digit percentage savings add up fast.

**4. What if my furnace stops working in the middle of winter?**
Don't panic — Module 4 covers this as an emergency procedure. The first-30-minutes checklist: check the thermostat (mode, setpoint, batteries), check the furnace power switch and breaker, check the filter (a severely clogged filter can trip safety shutoffs), and check that exterior intake/exhaust vents aren't blocked by snow or ice. If those don't resolve it — or if you smell gas or the CO detector is alarming — stop, and call for professional help immediately: your gas utility's emergency line for gas smells, 911 if there's any immediate danger, and an HVAC technician for the repair. The course also covers freeze protection while you wait: keeping pipes from freezing matters as much as staying warm, so you'll know the stopgaps (open cabinet doors under sinks, let faucets drip slightly, keep interior doors open for heat circulation).
