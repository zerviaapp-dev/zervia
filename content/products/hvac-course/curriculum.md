# Home HVAC Basics — Full Curriculum

*4 modules • self-paced • lifetime access. Every module marks the line between homeowner maintenance and licensed-technician work.*

> **Disclaimer:** This course is for educational purposes only. Gas and electrical work inside HVAC units must be performed by licensed technicians. Carbon monoxide safety is serious — install detectors on every floor and treat any alarm as an emergency.

---

## Module 1: How Heating & Cooling Works ✅ *(written — see module-1-how-it-works.md)*

The complete beginner baseline: what happens between "thermostat clicks" and "warm air from the vent" — and the same story in reverse for cooling.

1. **The Heating Cycle, Step by Step** — thermostat call → ignition/burner → heat exchanger warms up → blower pushes air across it → ducts carry warm air to rooms → return ducts bring cool air back. Follow one full cycle and the whole system clicks.
2. **Furnaces vs. Boilers vs. Heat Pumps** — how each one makes (or moves) heat, the pros and cons of each, and which one you likely have. Forced air vs. hydronic vs. refrigerant-based heating, in plain language.
3. **How Air Conditioning Works: The Refrigeration Cycle, Simply** — heat doesn't get "made cold" — it gets *moved*. The evaporator, condenser, compressor, and refrigerant loop explained with a sponge-and-bucket analogy you'll actually remember.
4. **Key Components and What Goes Wrong With Each** — thermostat, igniter, burners, heat exchanger, blower motor, filters, ductwork, condensate drain, refrigerant lines: what each part does, its typical failure mode, and whether it's your problem or a technician's.
5. **Efficiency Ratings Explained: AFUE and SEER** — what those stickers on your equipment mean, how to read them, and how they translate into real dollars on your energy bill — including what to look for when replacement day comes.

## Module 2: Filters & Maintenance

The highest-return maintenance in your entire home: cheap, fast, and skipped by almost everyone.

1. **Filter Types and MERV Ratings** — fiberglass vs. pleated vs. media filters; what MERV numbers actually measure; why a higher MERV isn't automatically better (airflow restriction is real); matching the filter to your system.
2. **How Often to Change Your Filter: The Real Answer** — the decision framework: filter thickness + MERV + pets + allergies + system runtime = your personal schedule. Why the "every 3 months" on the box is marketing, not engineering.
3. **Cleaning Supply and Return Vents** — vacuuming registers, keeping furniture and curtains clear, and why closing vents in unused rooms usually *hurts* efficiency instead of helping.
4. **Fall Furnace Checklist** — the pre-heating-season walkthrough: filter, vents, thermostat test, exterior intake/exhaust clear of debris and snow, CO detectors tested, and the area around the furnace clear.
5. **Spring AC Checklist** — the pre-cooling-season walkthrough: filter, outdoor condenser unit clear of vegetation and debris, condensate drain check, and thermostat cooling test before the first hot day.

## Module 3: Thermostats

The brain of the system — small device, outsized impact on comfort and bills.

1. **Thermostat Types: From Basic to Smart** — manual, programmable, Wi-Fi, and learning thermostats: what each one does, what each one costs, and who actually benefits from an upgrade.
2. **Programming Schedules That Match Real Life** — wake, away, home, sleep: building a schedule around your actual routine instead of the factory defaults nobody uses.
3. **Setback Strategies: How Low Can You Go?** — the science of setback savings, and the cold-climate limit: how far you can drop the temperature overnight or while away without risking frozen pipes (the Alberta rule of thumb).
4. **Smart Thermostats: What They Actually Do** — remote control, learning, geofencing, energy reports, and room sensors: separating genuine value from marketing. Compatibility check — not every smart thermostat works with every system.
5. **Thermostat Placement and Accuracy** — why a thermostat in direct sun, near a draft, or above a heat source lies to your furnace — and what to do about it.

## Module 4: Troubleshooting & Energy Saving

When something's wrong: the safe diagnostic steps, the clear line to a technician, and the winter emergency plan.

1. **No Heat: The Diagnostic Sequence** — thermostat settings and batteries → furnace power switch and breaker → filter condition → exterior vents blocked by snow/ice → pilot/ignition indicators. What each check tells you, in order, before you pick up the phone.
2. **Weak Airflow** — filter, blocked registers, duct dampers, blower issues: narrowing it down from the easy fixes to the "call a tech" ones.
3. **Strange Noises: What They're Telling You** — banging, squealing, rattling, humming: matching sounds to likely causes, and which ones mean "shut it down and call now."
4. **Short Cycling** — when the furnace turns on and off rapidly: the common causes (oversized system, clogged filter, thermostat issues) and why it wastes energy and wears equipment.
5. **When to Call a Technician: The Clear Line** — anything involving gas, burners, the heat exchanger, electrical components inside the unit, or refrigerant; any CO alarm; any smell of gas; anything you're unsure about after the safe checks. Plus: **Furnace-out-in-winter emergency guidance** — the first-30-minutes checklist and freeze protection while you wait for help.
6. **Energy-Saving Tips for Alberta Winters** — the stacked wins: setback strategy, filter discipline, sealing obvious duct leaks, weatherstripping, humidification for comfort at lower setpoints, and understanding when an aging furnace is costing more to run than to replace.
