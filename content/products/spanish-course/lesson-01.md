# Lesson 1: Greetings & Introductions

**Module 2 — Greetings & Introductions · Lesson 1**

Welcome to your first Spanish lesson! By the end of it, you'll be able to greet
anyone, introduce yourself, and say goodbye — the three things every conversation
needs. Let's go. *¡Vamos!*

---

## How to read the pronunciation guides

Spanish pronunciation is refreshingly consistent. A few ground rules before we start:

- Every letter is pronounced — there are no silent mysteries like in English
  (except **h**, which is always silent: *hola* = "OH-lah").
- Vowels always sound the same: **a** = "ah", **e** = "eh", **i** = "ee",
  **o** = "oh", **u** = "oo".
- Capital letters in the guides (OH, EE) mark the stressed syllable.

## 15 essential greetings & introductions

| # | Spanish | Pronunciation | English meaning | When to use it |
|---|---------|---------------|-----------------|----------------|
| 1 | Hola | **OH**-lah | Hello / Hi | Anytime, anyone — the safest word in Spanish |
| 2 | Buenos días | **BWEH**-nohs **DEE**-ahs | Good morning | Morning until about noon |
| 3 | Buenas tardes | **BWEH**-nahs **TAHR**-dehs | Good afternoon | Noon until sunset |
| 4 | Buenas noches | **BWEH**-nahs **NOH**-chehs | Good evening / Good night | Evening onward; also means "good night" at bedtime |
| 5 | ¿Cómo estás? | **KOH**-moh ehs-**TAHS** | How are you? (informal) | Friends, peers, people your age or younger |
| 6 | ¿Cómo está? | **KOH**-moh ehs-**TAH** | How are you? (formal) | Older people, strangers in formal settings, professionals |
| 7 | ¿Cómo está usted? | **KOH**-moh ehs-**TAH** oos-**TEHD** | How are you? (very formal) | Adds *usted* explicitly — extra respect, e.g., a doctor or official |
| 8 | Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú? | bee-**EHN**, **GRAH**-syahs. ee **TOO**? | Fine, thanks. And you? (informal) | The classic reply among friends |
| 9 | Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y usted? | moo-ee bee-**EHN**, **GRAH**-syahs. ee oos-**TEHD**? | Very well, thanks. And you? (formal) | The classic reply in formal settings |
| 10 | Me llamo… | meh **YAH**-moh… | My name is… (literally "I call myself…") | Introducing yourself — add your name |
| 11 | Soy… | **SOY**… | I am… | A shorter intro: *Soy María* ("I'm María") |
| 12 | ¿Cómo te llamas? | **KOH**-moh teh **YAH**-mahs? | What's your name? (informal) | Asking a friend or peer their name |
| 13 | ¿Cómo se llama? | **KOH**-moh seh **YAH**-mah? | What's your name? (formal) | Asking an older person or in formal settings |
| 14 | Encantado / Encantada | ehn-kahn-**TAH**-doh / ehn-kahn-**TAH**-dah | Delighted / Nice to meet you | Say *encantado* if you're male, *encantada* if female |
| 15 | Mucho gusto | **MOO**-choh **GOOS**-toh | Nice to meet you (literally "much pleasure") | Works for everyone, everywhere — a great default |

### Quick extras worth knowing now

- **Gracias** (*GRAH*-syahs) — Thank you. Use it constantly; politeness is currency.
- **Por favor** (pohr fah-**VOHR**) — Please. Softens every request.
- **Adiós** (ah-dee-**OHS**) — Goodbye. Slightly final — like you won't see them for a while.
- **Hasta luego** (**AH**-stah loo-**EH**-goh) — See you later. The everyday goodbye.
- **Nos vemos** (nohs **VEH**-mohs) — See you. Casual, friendly, literally "we see each other."
- **Igualmente** (ee-gwahl-**MEHN**-teh) — Likewise / Same to you. The perfect reply to
  *mucho gusto* or *encantado*.

## Tú vs. usted: the one rule that matters most

Spanish has two ways to say "you":

- **Tú** (too) — informal. Friends, family, children, people your age, coworkers
  in relaxed workplaces.
- **Usted** (oos-*TEHD*) — formal. Older adults, strangers in professional settings,
  officials, anyone you want to show respect to.

**The safe default:** when in doubt, start with **usted**. If the other person
wants informality, they'll switch to *tú* — and in Latin America many people will
invite you with *"tutéame"* ("use tú with me"). Starting formal is never rude;
starting informal with the wrong person can be.

**Regional note:** In parts of Latin America (Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, and
Central America), people use **vos** instead of *tú*. You'll be understood with
*tú* everywhere — just recognize *vos* when you hear it.

## Mini-dialogue 1: Meeting a friend

**María:** ¡Hola, Carlos! ¿Cómo estás?
*(OH-lah, KAHR-lohs! KOH-moh ehs-TAHS?)*
Hi, Carlos! How are you?

**Carlos:** ¡Hola, María! Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?
*(OH-lah, mah-REE-ah! moo-ee bee-EHN, GRAH-syahs. ee TOO?)*
Hi, María! Very well, thanks. And you?

**María:** Bien, gracias. ¡Nos vemos!
*(bee-EHN, GRAH-syahs. nohs VEH-mohs!)*
Fine, thanks. See you!

**Why it works:** Two friends, so everything is *tú* — *estás*, *y tú*. The
goodbye is casual (*nos vemos*), not final.

---

## Mini-dialogue 2: Meeting someone older (formal)

**Ana:** Buenos días, señor García. ¿Cómo está usted?
*(BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs, seh-NYOHR gahr-SEE-ah. KOH-moh ehs-TAH oos-TEHD?)*
Good morning, Mr. García. How are you?

**Sr. García:** Buenos días, Ana. Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y usted?
*(BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs, AH-nah. moo-ee bee-EHN, GRAH-syahs. ee oos-TEHD?)*
Good morning, Ana. Very well, thank you. And you?

**Ana:** Bien, gracias. Encantada de conocerle.
*(bee-EHN, GRAH-syahs. ehn-kahn-TAH-dah deh koh-noh-SEHR-leh.)*
Fine, thanks. Delighted to meet you.

**Why it works:** Ana uses *usted* forms throughout (*está*, *conocerle*) with an
older man she's just met. Note *encantada* — Ana is female, so she uses the
**-a** ending. *Señor* (Mr.) adds respect.

## Mini-dialogue 3: Introducing yourself at a gathering

**Diego:** Hola, me llamo Diego. ¿Cómo te llamas?
*(OH-lah, meh YAH-moh dee-EH-goh. KOH-moh teh YAH-mahs?)*
Hi, I'm Diego. What's your name?

**Lucía:** Hola, Diego. Soy Lucía. Mucho gusto.
*(OH-lah, dee-EH-goh. SOY loo-SEE-ah. MOO-choh GOOS-toh.)*
Hi, Diego. I'm Lucía. Nice to meet you.

**Diego:** Igualmente, Lucía. ¡Hasta luego!
*(ee-gwahl-MEHN-teh, loo-SEE-ah. AH-stah loo-EH-goh!)*
Likewise, Lucía. See you later!

**Why it works:** A relaxed social setting, so *tú* is fine (*te llamas*).
*Igualmente* is the natural, confident reply to *mucho gusto* — learn it now and
you'll use it forever.

## Practice exercise: build your own introduction

Do this out loud — Spanish lives in your mouth, not on the page.

1. **Say hello** three ways: *hola*, *buenos días*, *buenas tardes*.
2. **Introduce yourself** two ways: *Me llamo ___* and *Soy ___*.
3. **Ask someone's name** — informal (*¿Cómo te llamas?*) and formal
   (*¿Cómo se llama?*).
4. **Answer "how are you"** both ways: *Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?* and
   *Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y usted?*
5. **Say goodbye** three ways: *adiós*, *hasta luego*, *nos vemos*.
6. **Challenge:** Write a 4-line dialogue where you meet a neighbor for the
   first time. Decide: *tú* or *usted*? (Hint: a neighbor your parents' age —
   start with *usted*.)

**Self-check:** Can you say all 15 phrases from the table without looking?
Cover the Spanish column and test yourself. Getting 12+ means you're ready for
Lesson 2.

## Cultural note: greetings are a ritual, not a formality

In most Spanish-speaking cultures, greeting people properly matters more than in
English-speaking ones. Walking into a small shop and saying *buenos días* to the
shopkeeper isn't optional politeness — it's expected, and skipping it can come
across as cold. Among friends and family, expect physical warmth: a cheek kiss
(one in most of Latin America, two in Spain), a hug, or a firm handshake in
formal settings. Don't rush the greeting to "get to business" — the *¿cómo estás?*
and the small talk *are* the business. Take your time, smile, and you'll be
welcomed like family.
