# Learn Spanish — Course Outline

**From Zero to Conversational: A Practical Spanish Course for English Speakers**

Taught entirely in English. No boring grammar drills — every lesson is built around
real conversations you'll actually have. By the end, you'll hold your own in
restaurants, shops, taxis, hotels, and friendly chats with native speakers.

---

## Module 1 — Pronunciation & the Alphabet
Sound natural from day one. Spanish is beautifully phonetic: what you see is
what you say.

1. **The Spanish alphabet** — the letters and their names, including ñ and ll.
2. **The 5 vowel sounds** — the foundation of good Spanish pronunciation; each
   vowel always sounds the same.
3. **Consonants that fool English speakers** — j, h (silent!), g, c, z, and the
   rolling r vs. the single r.
4. **Stress rules & accent marks** — where the emphasis falls and why á, é, í
   matter (cántara vs. cantara is NOT the same word).
5. **Your first 50 words** — cognates: hotel, taxi, doctor, música — words you
   already know.
6. **Tongue twisters & listening drills** — fun practice to train your ear.

## Module 2 — Greetings & Introductions
Walk into any room and make a good first impression.

1. **Hello, good morning, good evening** — hola, buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches.
2. **Introducing yourself** — me llamo, soy, and how to ask someone's name.
3. **Tú vs. usted: the big decision** — when to be formal and when to be friendly.
4. **Nice to meet you** — encantado/a, igualmente, mucho gusto.
5. **How are you, really** — ¿cómo estás? vs. ¿cómo está? and honest answers.
6. **Saying goodbye properly** — adiós, hasta luego, nos vemos, and what each implies.

## Module 3 — Numbers, Time & Dates
The math of daily life: money, schedules, reservations, appointments.

1. **Numbers 1–100** — built step by step, with the quirky teens (dieciséis).
2. **Big numbers: hundreds, thousands, millions** — cien vs. ciento, mil, millón.
3. **Telling the time** — son las tres, y media, menos cuarto, and o'clock logic.
4. **Days, months, seasons** — and why they're lowercase in Spanish.
5. **Dates and appointments** — el cinco de mayo, and booking anything on the phone.
6. **Money amounts in the wild** — prices, bills, and bargaining numbers.
7. **Practice drills** — read a receipt, set a meeting, catch a bus time.

## Module 4 — Food & Dining
Order anything, anywhere, with confidence — and enjoy the social ritual of the table.

1. **At the restaurant: getting a table** — mesa para dos, reservations, waiting.
2. **Ordering food and drinks** — quiero, me gustaría, para mí; reading a menu.
3. **Food vocabulary essentials** — meats, vegetables, sides, and common dishes.
4. **Dietary needs & allergies** — soy vegetariano/a, sin gluten, estoy alérgico/a.
5. **The bill, tips, and compliments** — la cuenta, por favor; tipping customs by country.
6. **Street food & markets** — ordering at a taco stand, a food truck, or a café counter.
7. **Sobremesa: the culture of lingering** — why nobody rushes you off the table.

## Module 5 — Shopping & Money
Markets, malls, and mercadillos — buy what you need at fair prices.

1. **Browsing and asking for help** — ¿puedo ayudarle? and how to say "just looking."
2. **Prices and haggling** — ¿cuánto cuesta? and bargaining politely in markets.
3. **Sizes, colors, and trying things on** — clothing and shoe vocabulary.
4. **Paying** — cash, card, and ¿puedo pagar con tarjeta?
5. **Exchanges and returns** — ¿puedo cambiar esto? and receipt phrases.
6. **Market Spanish** — kilos, rebajas, and the vocabulary of open-air markets.

## Module 6 — Travel & Directions
Airports, taxis, hotels, and finding your way without panic.

1. **At the airport** — check-in, boarding, delays, lost luggage phrases.
2. **Taxis and rideshares** — giving an address, asking the fare, haggling politely.
3. **Asking for and understanding directions** — ¿dónde está…? plus left/right/straight vocab.
4. **Public transport** — buses, metros, tickets, and asking which stop.
5. **At the hotel** — check-in, room requests, problems, check-out.
6. **Emergencies on the road** — car trouble, lost passport, finding the police.

## Module 7 — Family & Social Life
Talk about the people in your life and actually enjoy small talk.

1. **Family vocabulary** — parents, siblings, in-laws, and the big Latin family.
2. **Describing people** — looks, personality, and the verbs ser vs. estar (finally, painlessly).
3. **Making plans with friends** — ¿qué haces el sábado? invitations and RSVPs.
4. **Small talk that works** — weather, weekend, football, food — safe topics that bond.
5. **Talking about your past** — where you're from, what you did — the preterite, conversationally.
6. **Phones and messaging** — texting abbreviations, voice notes, and call etiquette.

## Module 8 — Work & Business Phrases
Polite, professional Spanish for the office, the job site, and video calls.

1. **Professional introductions** — titles, roles, and usted by default.
2. **Meetings and presentations** — opening, opinions, agreeing, disagreeing politely.
3. **Email and message templates** — formal closings (atentamente) that don't sound stiff.
4. **Phone calls in Spanish** — holding, transferring, taking a message.
5. **Negotiating and scheduling** — follow-ups, deadlines, and ¿podríamos…?
6. **Industry quick-phrases** — construction, hospitality, healthcare, retail starter sets.

## Module 9 — Emergencies & Health
The module you hope to never use — learn it first anyway.

1. **Getting help fast** — ¡ayuda! ¡socorro! and calling emergency numbers by country.
2. **At the doctor** — describing pain and symptoms: me duele, tengo fiebre.
3. **At the pharmacy** — prescriptions, dosages, and over-the-counter requests.
4. **At the hospital** — allergies, medications, insurance phrases.
5. **Police and legal basics** — reporting theft, lost documents, getting a report.
6. **Travel insurance & documents** — the paperwork phrases that unlock everything.

## Module 10 — Culture, Etiquette & Conversation Practice
Sound like a person, not a phrasebook. Then put it all together.

1. **Tú vs. usted, mastered** — age, region, and context rules across 20+ countries.
2. **Latin America vs. Spain** — vosotros, coger (careful!), z vs. s, and vocab that changes
   at the border: carro/coche, computadora/ordenador, jugo/zumo.
3. **Greetings with touch** — the cheek kiss: one, two, or none — country by country.
4. **Politeness that matters** — por favor, gracias, and the art of softening requests.
5. **10 full conversations** — restaurant, hotel, taxi, market, doctor, phone call, job
   interview, party, directions, small talk — with breakdowns.
6. **Your 30-day practice plan** — daily 15-minute routines to keep the Spanish alive
   after the course ends.
