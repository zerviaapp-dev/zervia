## What you'll actually be able to do

Not "understand the fundamentals of Sino-Tibetan linguistics." Real things:

- **Greet anyone** — from a taxi driver (你好 nǐ hǎo) to a business client (您好 nín hǎo) — and know exactly which one fits
- **Order food with confidence** — point, customize, handle the check: 我要这个 (wǒ yào zhège), 买单 (mǎidān)
- **Shop and bargain** — ask prices, haggle politely, and pay like a local: 多少钱？(duōshao qián?)
- **Get around** — give directions to a driver, ask strangers for help, check into hotels
- **Handle the hard moments** — emergencies, doctors, lost passports: the phrases you hope you'll never need
- **Do business respectfully** — exchange cards the right way, understand guanxi (关系), and navigate the business dinner

## Why this course works when apps didn't

**🎯 Conversational, not academic.** No grammar drills. No memorizing stroke order before you can say hello. Every lesson is built around a real situation — a restaurant, a market stall, a first meeting — so what you learn is what you'll use.

**🎵 Tones made approachable.** Yes, the four tones are the hardest part of Mandarin, and we won't pretend otherwise. But they're also *learnable* — with the hand-gesture method, tone-pair drills, and ear training that turns "they all sound the same" into "oh, I hear it now." Most students get comfortable with tones in the first two weeks.

**🏮 Culture in every module.** Language without culture is just noise. You'll learn *why* you receive a business card with both hands, *why* direct "no" is rare in negotiations, and *why* tapping the table means "thank you" at tea. These are the details that turn a phrasebook tourist into someone people genuinely enjoy talking to.

**📖 Real-life scenarios, start to finish.** Each module ends with a role-play: the market negotiation, the lost tourist, the first business meeting, the emergency drill. You don't just learn phrases — you rehearse using them under (friendly) pressure.
