# Lesson 1: Greetings & Introductions
### Module 2 — Your first real Mandarin conversations

*By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to greet anyone, introduce yourself, ask someone's name, and say goodbye — the four moves that start every relationship.*

**How to read this lesson:** Every phrase is shown as **Chinese characters** + *pinyin* (with tone marks) + English. The tone marks tell you the pitch: ā (high flat), á (rising), ǎ (dip down then up), à (falling). No mark = neutral tone (short and relaxed). Don't stress about perfection — just try to match the "melody" of each phrase.

## Part 1 — The Essentials: 15 phrases that open every door

### Saying hello

| # | Characters | Pinyin | English | When to use it |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|----------------|
| 1 | 你好 | *nǐ hǎo* | Hello | Your all-purpose greeting — friendly, neutral, works almost everywhere. (Note: spoken, the first third tone shifts: *ní hǎo*.) |
| 2 | 您好 | *nín hǎo* | Hello (respectful) | For elders, clients, officials — anyone you want to show respect to. The 您 (*nín*) is the polite "you." |
| 3 | 早上好 | *zǎoshang hǎo* | Good morning | Before ~10am. Warm and a little formal — great for workplaces. |
| 4 | 晚上好 | *wǎnshang hǎo* | Good evening | After sunset. Used at events, restaurants, evening meetings. |
| 5 | 你好吗? | *nǐ hǎo ma?* | How are you? | Casual and friendly. Among close friends, 最近怎么样? (*zuìjìn zěnmeyàng?* — "How have you been lately?") sounds more natural. |

### Introducing yourself

| # | Characters | Pinyin | English | When to use it |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|----------------|
| 6 | 我叫... | *wǒ jiào...* | My name is... (lit. "I am called...") | The standard self-introduction. 我叫李明 (*wǒ jiào Lǐ Míng*) — "My name is Li Ming." |
| 7 | 我是... | *wǒ shì...* | I am... | For roles and affiliations: 我是工程师 (*wǒ shì gōngchéngshī*) — "I am an engineer." |
| 8 | 你叫什么名字? | *nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?* | What's your name? | 什么 (*shénme*) = "what." Use with peers and in casual settings. |
| 9 | 请问您贵姓? | *qǐngwèn nín guìxìng?* | May I ask your (honorable) surname? | The polite, formal version — perfect for business. 贵姓 (*guìxìng*) literally means "honorable surname." |
| 10 | 很高兴认识你 | *hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ* | Nice to meet you | Say it with a smile and a nod (or handshake). Swap 你 for 您 (*nín*) for extra respect. |

### Politeness pillars

| # | Characters | Pinyin | English | When to use it |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|----------------|
| 11 | 谢谢 | *xièxie* | Thank you | Use it freely — more often than in English feels natural. Add 你 (*nǐ*) or 您 (*nín*) for warmth: 谢谢你! |
| 12 | 不客气 | *bú kèqi* | You're welcome | The reply to 谢谢. (Note the tone change: 不 *bù* becomes *bú* before a fourth tone.) |
| 13 | 对不起 | *duìbuqǐ* | Sorry / excuse me (apology) | Bumping into someone, being late, any real apology. |
| 14 | 没关系 | *méi guānxi* | It's okay / no problem | The reply to 对不起 — instantly defuses awkwardness. |
| 15 | 请问 | *qǐngwèn* | Excuse me (to ask something) | Your opener for any question to a stranger: 请问, 洗手间在哪里? (*qǐngwèn, xǐshǒujiān zài nǎlǐ?* — "Excuse me, where is the restroom?") |

### Saying goodbye

| # | Characters | Pinyin | English | When to use it |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|----------------|
| — | 再见 | *zàijiàn* | Goodbye | The standard farewell — literally "again see." Works everywhere. |
| — | 拜拜 | *bàibai* | Bye-bye | Casual, friendly — borrowed from English, used with friends and peers. |

## Part 2 — Usage notes: the details that make you sound natural

**你 (*nǐ*) vs. 您 (*nín*) — the respect switch.** 你 is the everyday "you" for friends, peers, and anyone younger. 您 is the respectful "you" for elders, clients, teachers, and officials. Rule of thumb: when in doubt, use 您 — nobody is ever offended by extra respect, but the reverse isn't true. In business settings, 您 is the default.

**Names go family-name first.** If someone says 我叫王芳 (*wǒ jiào Wáng Fāng*), 王 (Wáng) is the family name and 芳 (Fāng) is the given name. Address people by family name + title (王先生 *Wáng xiānsheng* — "Mr. Wang") until invited to do otherwise.

**Greetings are lighter than in English.** Chinese speakers don't "How are you?" every stranger the way English speakers do. A simple 你好 with eye contact and a nod carries the whole interaction. Save 你好吗? for people you actually know.

**The third-tone shift (a free upgrade to your accent).** When two third tones meet, the first becomes a second tone: 你好 *nǐ hǎo* is pronounced *ní hǎo*. You'll hear this everywhere — mimicking it is the fastest way to sound less like a textbook.

## Part 3 — Three mini-dialogues

### Dialogue 1: Meeting a colleague

> **A:** 你好!我叫大卫,你叫什么名字?
> *Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ jiào Dàwèi, nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?*
> Hello! I'm David — what's your name?
>
> **B:** 你好,大卫!我叫陈丽。很高兴认识你。
> *Nǐ hǎo, Dàwèi! Wǒ jiào Chén Lì. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.*
> Hello, David! I'm Chen Li. Nice to meet you.
>
> **A:** 很高兴认识你!
> *Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ!*
> Nice to meet you too!

### Dialogue 2: A first business meeting

> **A:** 您好,请问您贵姓?
> *Nín hǎo, qǐngwèn nín guìxìng?*
> Hello — may I ask your surname?
>
> **B:** 您好,我姓林。这是我的名片。
> *Nín hǎo, wǒ xìng Lín. Zhè shì wǒ de míngpiàn.*
> Hello, my surname is Lin. This is my business card. *(offered with both hands)*
>
> **A:** 谢谢,林先生。很高兴认识您。
> *Xièxie, Lín xiānsheng. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nín.*
> Thank you, Mr. Lin. Very nice to meet you. *(receives card with both hands, reads it)*
>
> **B:** 不客气!
> *Bú kèqi!*
> You're welcome!

### Dialogue 3: Leaving a dinner

> **A:** 谢谢你的晚餐,非常好吃!
> *Xièxie nǐ de wǎncān, fēicháng hǎochī!*
> Thank you for dinner — it was delicious!
>
> **B:** 不客气,下次再来!
> *Bú kèqi, xià cì zài lái!*
> You're welcome — come again next time!
>
> **A:** 好的,再见!
> *Hǎo de, zàijiàn!*
> Sounds good — goodbye!
>
> **B:** 再见,路上小心!
> *Zàijiàn, lùshang xiǎoxīn!*
> Goodbye — travel safe! *(lit. "be careful on the road")*

## Part 5 — Cultural note: business cards & guanxi basics

**The business card ritual.** In Chinese business culture, the exchange of名片 (*míngpiàn* — business cards) is a small ceremony, not a casual handoff:

- **Offer and receive with BOTH hands**, card facing the other person so they can read it.
- **Read it carefully** before putting it away — glancing and pocketing signals you don't value the person.
- **Never write on someone's card** or shove it in a back pocket in front of them. Place it on the table during the meeting or into a card case.
- Present your own card with your details facing them, and offer a slight nod as you do.

**Guanxi (关系) — relationships are the business.** *Guanxi* literally means "relationships," but in business it means something deeper: the network of trust and mutual obligation that makes things happen. Deals in China are rarely closed on the first meeting — they're closed at the third dinner. The greetings in this lesson aren't just politeness; they're the first deposits in a guanxi account. Take them seriously, and doors open.
