# Speak Mandarin: From Your First 你好 to Real Conversations
### Complete Course Curriculum — Beginner to Conversational

**Course language of instruction:** English (for English speakers)
**Format:** Pinyin-first approach — every phrase is shown as Chinese characters + pinyin + English, so you speak from day one before touching character memorization.
**Philosophy:** Conversational and practical. You learn the phrases real people actually use in restaurants, shops, taxis, and meetings — not academic grammar drills.

---

## MODULE 1 — Pinyin & The Four Tones (The Foundation)
*Everything in Mandarin starts here. The tones feel strange for a week, then they click.*

1. **What Mandarin actually sounds like** — A tour of the language: why pinyin exists and how it makes Mandarin learnable for English speakers.
2. **The four tones, made simple** — mā (mother) / má (hemp) / mǎ (horse) / mà (scold) — hearing and producing each tone, with the hand-gesture trick that makes them stick.
3. **The neutral tone (轻声 qīngshēng)** — The "relaxed" fifth tone: xièxie 谢谢, māma 妈妈 — when a syllable goes soft and unstressed.
4. **Pinyin initials you already know** — b, p, m, f, d, t, n, l map closely to English — quick wins to build confidence.
5. **The tricky ones: j, q, x / zh, ch, sh / z, c, s** — Tongue-position diagrams in words; the sounds that don't exist in English, broken down one at a time.
6. **Tone pairs & tone sandhi (变调 biàndiào)** — Why 你好 is spoken nǐ hǎo → ní hǎo; the third-tone rule and the 不/一 tone changes that make you sound natural.
7. **Your first 20 spoken words** — Everyday words (人 rén, 好 hǎo, 大 dà, 小 xiǎo, 水 shuǐ...) drilled in tone pairs so your mouth learns the music.
8. **Tone ear-training workout** — Listening drills: same syllable, different tones — train your ear to hear the difference before Module 2.

## MODULE 2 — Greetings & Introductions
*Meet people, make a good first impression, and start every relationship right.*

1. **Hello the right way: 你好 nǐ hǎo vs 您好 nín hǎo** — Casual vs. respectful; when to use which (hint: nín for elders, clients, anyone you want to impress).
2. **"My name is..." — 我叫 wǒ jiào & 我是 wǒ shì** — Introducing yourself; asking 你叫什么名字？(nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?) — "What's your name?"
3. **Nice to meet you: 很高兴认识你 hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ** — The warm standard greeting for new acquaintances, plus natural follow-ups.
4. **How are you, really?** — 你好吗？(nǐ hǎo ma?), 你怎么样？(nǐ zěnmeyàng?), and why 最近怎么样？(zuìjìn zěnmeyàng?) sounds more natural among friends.
5. **Good morning / good evening** — 早上好 (zǎoshang hǎo), 晚上好 (wǎnshang hǎo) — time-of-day greetings and when Chinese speakers actually use them.
6. **Goodbyes that land well** — 再见 (zàijiàn), 拜拜 (bàibai), 慢走 (màn zǒu) — matching your farewell to the situation.
7. **Please, thank you, sorry, excuse me** — 请 (qǐng), 谢谢 (xièxie), 对不起 (duìbuqǐ), 没关系 (méi guānxi), 请问 (qǐngwèn) — the five politeness pillars.
8. **Mini-dialogues: your first real conversations** — Three scripted exchanges (meeting a colleague, greeting a host, leaving a dinner) with line-by-line breakdowns.

## MODULE 3 — Numbers, Time & Dates
*Count, tell time, and handle money — the most-used vocabulary in any language.*

1. **Numbers 1–10, then to 100** — 一到十 (yī dào shí): the tones of numbers, and the pattern that makes 11–99 automatic.
2. **Big numbers: hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands** — The 万 (wàn) system: why 10,000 is one unit and how prices are really quoted.
3. **Telling time** — 现在几点？(xiànzài jǐ diǎn?) — 点/分/刻/半 (diǎn/fèn/kè/bàn): hours, minutes, quarter-hours, and half-hours.
4. **Days, weeks, months, years** — 星期一到星期天 (xīngqī yī dào xīngqī tiān); 月份 and 号/日 for dates — saying "October 9th" without thinking.
5. **Yesterday, today, tomorrow** — 昨天/今天/明天 (zuótiān/jīntiān/míngtiān) plus 上/下 week and month expressions.
6. **Money & prices** — 多少钱？(duōshao qián?) — 块/元 (kuài/yuán), 毛/角 (máo/jiǎo) — hearing and saying prices in markets and shops.
7. **Phone numbers & addresses** — Digit-by-digit reading (一 yāo in phone numbers), and how addresses run big-to-small (country → street).
8. **Practice: the market stall drill** — A full role-play: asking prices, bargaining, and counting change — numbers under pressure.

## MODULE 4 — Food & Dining
*Order confidently, survive a banquet, and never accidentally eat something you didn't want.*

1. **The essential food vocabulary** — 米饭 (mǐfàn), 面条 (miàntiáo), 菜 (cài), 肉 (ròu), 水 (shuǐ), 茶 (chá) — the 20 words that cover 80% of menus.
2. **Ordering like a local** — 我要这个 (wǒ yào zhège) "I'll have this one," 来一份 (lái yí fèn), and pointing strategies when the menu is Chinese-only.
3. **Flavors & preferences** — 辣 (là) spicy, 甜 (tián) sweet, 咸 (xián) salty, 少放 (shǎo fàng) "less of..." — customizing your dish.
4. **Dietary needs & allergies** — 我不吃... (wǒ bù chī...) "I don't eat...", 我对...过敏 (wǒ duì... guòmǐn) "I'm allergic to..." — critical safety phrases.
5. **At the restaurant: the full flow** — From 几位？(jǐ wèi? "how many people?") to 买单 (mǎidān) "the check, please" — the whole meal in Mandarin.
6. **Tea culture basics** — 喝茶 (hē chá): tapping the table to say thanks, refilling others before yourself.
7. **Banquet survival guide** — 干杯 (gānbēi) toasts, seating hierarchy, and why the host orders for the table.
8. **Street food & night markets** — 好吃 (hǎochī) "delicious!", 太贵了 (tài guì le) "too expensive!", and bargaining for snacks.

## MODULE 5 — Shopping & Money
*Bargain with a smile, pay with your phone, and never get the tourist price twice.*

1. **Shopping vocabulary** — 买 (mǎi) buy, 卖 (mài) sell, 商店 (shāngdiàn) shop, 便宜 (piányi) cheap, 贵 (guì) expensive.
2. **"How much is this?" and the bargaining dance** — 多少钱？(duōshao qián?), 能便宜一点吗？(néng piányi yìdiǎn ma?) — the polite haggle, step by step.
3. **Sizes, colors, trying things on** — 大/小/中 (dà/xiǎo/zhōng), 颜色 (yánsè), 我可以试穿吗？(wǒ kěyǐ shìchuān ma?) "Can I try this on?"
4. **Paying: cash, card, and mobile** — 现金 (xiànjīn), 刷卡 (shuākǎ), 微信支付/支付宝 (Wēixìn zhīfù/Zhīfùbǎo) — "我用...付" (wǒ yòng... fù).
5. **"I'm just looking"** — 我随便看看 (wǒ suíbiàn kànkan), and the graceful exits when the shopkeeper follows you.
6. **Returns & problems** — 可以退吗？(kěyǐ tuì ma?) "Can I return this?", 坏了 (huài le) "it's broken" — handling issues calmly.
7. **Online shopping phrases** — 淘宝/京东 (Táobǎo/Jīngdōng) basics: 包邮 (bāoyóu) free shipping, 优惠券 (yōuhuìquàn) coupons.
8. **Role-play: the full market negotiation** — From opening price to handshake, with three difficulty levels.

## MODULE 6 — Travel & Directions
*Get anywhere, ask anyone, and handle the hiccups that come with the road.*

1. **Getting around: transport vocabulary** — 出租车 (chūzūchē) taxi, 地铁 (dìtiě) subway, 公交车 (gōngjiāochē) bus, 飞机 (fēijī) plane, 火车 (huǒchē) train.
2. **"Take me to..."** — 去... (qù...), 师傅，去... (shīfu, qù...) — giving taxi drivers addresses and confirming the route.
3. **Asking for directions** — 请问，...怎么走？(qǐngwèn, ... zěnme zǒu?) — understanding 左/右/直走 (zuǒ/yòu/zhí zǒu): left, right, go straight.
4. **Landmarks & distances** — 远 (yuǎn) far, 近 (jìn) near, 旁边 (pángbiān) next to, 对面 (duìmiàn) across from.
5. **At the hotel** — 我有预订 (wǒ yǒu yùdìng) "I have a reservation," 入住/退房 (rùzhù/tuìfáng) check-in/check-out, and common requests.
6. **At the airport & train station** — 登机口 (dēngjīkǒu) gate, 晚点 (wǎndiǎn) delayed, 护照 (hùzhào) passport — navigating transit hubs.
7. **"I need help"** — 我迷路了 (wǒ mílù le) "I'm lost," 你能帮我吗？(nǐ néng bāng wǒ ma?) "Can you help me?"
8. **Role-play: the lost tourist** — A full scenario: wrong subway stop, asking strangers, and arriving with a story.

## MODULE 7 — Family & Social Life
*Talk about the people who matter — family is everything in Chinese culture.*

1. **Family members** — 爸爸/妈妈 (bàba/māma), 哥哥/姐姐/弟弟/妹妹 (gēge/jiějie/dìdi/mèimei) — and why Chinese has different words for older vs. younger siblings.
2. **"Do you have...?"** — 你有孩子吗？(nǐ yǒu háizi ma?) — asking about family the natural, warm way.
3. **Talking about yourself** — 我住在... (wǒ zhù zài...) "I live in...", 我的工作是... (wǒ de gōngzuò shì...) "My job is..." — the small-talk toolkit.
4. **Hobbies & weekends** — 你喜欢做什么？(nǐ xǐhuan zuò shénme?) — sports, food, travel, and finding common ground.
5. **Making plans** — 你有空吗？(nǐ yǒu kòng ma?) "Are you free?", 我们一起吃饭吧 (wǒmen yìqǐ chīfàn ba) "Let's eat together."
6. **Invitations & gifts** — The art of insisting (and refusing politely): 来嘛！(lái ma!), 不用客气 (bú yòng kèqi).
7. **WeChat: the social lifeline** — 加个微信吧 (jiā ge Wēixìn ba) "Let's add each other on WeChat" — exchanging contacts the modern way.
8. **Festivals & celebrations** — 春节 (Chūnjié) Spring Festival, 中秋节 (Zhōngqiūjié) Mid-Autumn Festival — what to say and when: 新年快乐！(xīnnián kuàilè!).

## MODULE 8 — Work & Business Phrases
*Do business with confidence — from the first handshake to the signed deal.*

1. **Professional introductions** — 这是我的名片 (zhè shì wǒ de míngpiàn) "This is my business card" — titles, companies, and roles: 我是...公司的... (wǒ shì... gōngsī de...).
2. **Business card etiquette (名片礼仪)** — Receive with BOTH hands, read it carefully, never write on it or pocket it immediately — the ritual that signals respect.
3. **Guanxi (关系): relationships are the business** — What guanxi really means, why dinners matter more than decks, and how trust is built over time.
4. **Meetings & small talk** — 开会 (kāihuì) meeting, 合作 (hézuò) cooperation — the phrases that open and warm up a business conversation.
5. **Negotiating politely** — 我们考虑一下 (wǒmen kǎolǜ yíxià) "We'll consider it," direct "no" is rare — reading between the lines.
6. **Emails & phone calls** — 您好，我是... (nín hǎo, wǒ shì...) phone openers, and the formal written phrases that show professionalism.
7. **Dining with clients** — Seating order, toasting etiquette (干杯 gānbēi), and who pays — the unwritten rules of the business meal.
8. **Role-play: the first meeting** — Full scenario: greeting, card exchange, small talk, and a graceful close.

## MODULE 9 — Emergencies & Health
*The module you hope you never need — and will be grateful to have.*

1. **Emergency essentials** — 救命！(jiùmìng!) "Help!", 报警 (bàojǐng) call the police, 110 / 120 / 119 — the numbers that matter.
2. **"I need a doctor"** — 我需要医生 (wǒ xūyào yīshēng), 医院在哪里？(yīyuàn zài nǎlǐ?) "Where is the hospital?"
3. **Describing symptoms** — 我头疼 (wǒ tóu téng) "my head hurts," 发烧 (fāshāo) fever, 肚子疼 (dùzi téng) stomachache — the body-part + pain pattern.
4. **At the pharmacy** — 药店 (yàodiàn), 感冒药 (gǎnmào yào) cold medicine — buying what you need without a prescription maze.
5. **Allergies & conditions** — 我对...过敏 (wǒ duì... guòmǐn), 我有... (wǒ yǒu...) "I have [condition]" — the medical alert card phrases.
6. **"I lost my..."** — 我的护照丢了 (wǒ de hùzhào diū le) "I lost my passport," 钱包 (qiánbāo) wallet, 手机 (shǒujī) phone.
7. **Asking for help from strangers** — 请帮帮我 (qǐng bāngbang wǒ) "Please help me" — getting attention kindly and effectively.
8. **Practice: the emergency drill** — Timed recall of the 10 most critical phrases — because in an emergency, hesitation costs.

## MODULE 10 — Culture, Etiquette & Conversation Practice
*Speak the language AND read the room — then put it all together.*

1. **Face (面子 miànzi)** — Why public correction and direct refusal are avoided; how to disagree without anyone losing face.
2. **The humility dance** — deflecting compliments (哪里哪里 nǎli nǎli), downplaying your own achievements — modesty as social grammar.
3. **Dining etiquette deep-dive** — Chopstick taboos (never stick them upright in rice!), serving others first, the last-bite rule.
4. **Visiting someone's home** — 带礼物 (dài lǐwù) bring a gift, 换鞋 (huàn xié) change shoes — being the guest everyone invites back.
5. **Lucky & unlucky: numbers, colors, gifts** — 8 is lucky, 4 is avoided, never gift clocks (送钟 sòng zhōng sounds like 送终 "funeral") or sharp objects.
6. **Conversation connectors** — 所以 (suǒyǐ) so, 但是 (dànshì) but, 然后 (ránhòu) then — the glue that turns phrases into flowing speech.
7. **Keeping a conversation alive** — Follow-up questions, reactions (真的吗？zhēn de ma? "really?!"), and the art of listening actively.
8. **Final challenge: your 5-minute conversation** — A guided capstone: introduce yourself, small talk, order food, ask directions, and say goodbye — entirely in Mandarin.
