## FAQ

**1. How long does it take to prepare?**
Honestly: **2 to 4 months** of consistent study for most working adults. The course is built around a 10–12 week plan at roughly 8–10 hours per week. If you're a recent grad with the math fresh, you may be ready in 6–8 weeks. If you've been out of school a decade, give yourself the full 4 months — there's no shame in the longer runway, and rushing is the #1 reason people fail.

**2. Is this course enough to pass on its own?**
This is a **structured accelerator**, not a magic pass. It gives you strategy, review, practice, and a plan — everything except the doing. We strongly recommend pairing it with the **official NCEES FE practice exam**, which is the single most representative material available. Think of this course as your coach and the NCEES materials as your sparring partner. Do both, and you're genuinely prepared.

**3. Which FE exam version does this cover?**
The course has an **FE Civil emphasis** — statics, mechanics of materials, fluid mechanics, and civil-flavored breadth topics. That said, Modules 1, 2, and 6 (strategy, mathematics, test-day playbook) apply to **every FE discipline**, and the problem-solving approach transfers directly. If you're taking FE Mechanical, Electrical, or another version, the morning-breadth coverage and strategy modules will still serve you well.

**4. What if I fail the exam?**
First: it happens, and it says nothing about your engineering ability — the FE is a *test-taking* skill as much as a knowledge test. If you don't pass, your diagnostic report shows exactly which topics dragged your score down. Retake strategy: **don't re-study everything.** Target only your bottom 3–4 topic areas, run timed drills on those for 3–4 weeks, re-take the NCEES practice exam, and rebook. Most retakers who study *surgically* pass on the second attempt. This course's Module 5 question sets and Module 1 study-plan framework are built for exactly this.
