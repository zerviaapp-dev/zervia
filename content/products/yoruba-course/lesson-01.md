# Lesson 1 — Greetings & Introductions

Welcome! In Yoruba culture, greeting is not small talk — it's the doorway to every relationship. Greet well, and doors open. This lesson gives you the essential greetings and introductions to start real conversations from day one.

> **About the marks:** Yoruba is tonal. The marks show pitch: **á** = high tone, **a** = mid tone (unmarked), **à** = low tone. The dots below letters matter too: **ẹ** sounds like "eh" in "bed," **ọ** like "aw" in "law," and **ṣ** like "sh" in "she."

---

## 15 essential greetings & introductions

### Time-of-day greetings

**1. Ẹ káàárọ̀** *(eh kah-ah-ROH)*
"Good morning" — to an elder or a group. The respectful form. *Ẹ* is the plural/respectful "you."

**2. Káàárọ̀** *(kah-ah-ROH)*
"Good morning" — to a friend or someone younger. Same greeting, familiar form.

**3. Ẹ káàsán** *(eh kah-ah-SAHN)*
"Good afternoon" — respectful form, used roughly midday to late afternoon.

**4. Káàsán o** *(kah-ah-SAHN oh)*
"Good afternoon" — familiar form. The little **o** at the end is a friendly touch.

**5. Ẹ káalẹ́** *(eh kah-ah-LEH)*
"Good evening" — respectful form, used from late afternoon into the night.

**6. Káalẹ́ o** *(kah-ah-LEH oh)*
"Good evening" — familiar form.

### How are you & responses

**7. Báwo ni?** *(BAH-woh nee)*
"How are you?" — the universal check-in. Works everywhere, with everyone.

**8. Báwo ni ń lọ?** *(BAH-woh nee n loh)*
"How is it going?" — a warmer, more conversational version of Báwo ni?

**9. Mo wà dáadáa** *(moh wah dah-ah-DAH-ah)*
"I'm doing well." — your go-to positive answer. People will smile when they hear this.

**10. Àlàáfíà** *(ah-lah-ah-FEE-ah)*
"Peace / all is well." — a calm, gracious answer, especially to elders.

### Thank you, welcome, goodbye

**11. Ẹ ṣeun** *(eh sheh-OON)*
"Thank you" — respectful form. Say this to elders, shopkeepers, hosts. Gratitude is a big deal in Yoruba culture.

**12. Ó ṣeun** *(oh sheh-OON)*
"Thank you" — familiar form, for friends and peers.

**13. Ẹ káàbọ̀** *(eh kah-ah-BAW)*
"Welcome" — respectful form, said to someone arriving.

**14. Ó dàa** *(oh dah-AH)*
"Goodbye" (literally "it is good"). Short, warm, everyday.

**15. Ó di ìgbà mìíràn** *(oh dee eeg-BAH mee-RAHN)*
"Until another time" — a lovely, warmer goodbye when you'll meet again.

### Introducing yourself (bonus phrases)

- **Orúkọ mi ni ___** *(oh-ROO-kaw mee nee…)* — "My name is ___."
- **Kí ni orúkọ rẹ?** *(kee nee oh-ROO-kaw reh?)* — "What is your name?" (familiar)
- **Kí ni orúkọ yín?** *(kee nee oh-ROO-kaw yeen?)* — "What is your name?" (respectful)
- **Níbo ni o ti wá?** *(NEE-baw nee oh tee wah?)* — "Where are you from?"
- **Mo ti ___ wá** *(moh tee … wah)* — "I am from ___."

## Mini-dialogue 1 — Morning greeting (with an elder)

**You:** Ẹ káàárọ̀, màmá.
*Good morning, ma.*

**Elder:** Káàárọ̀. Báwo ni?
*Good morning. How are you?*

**You:** Mo wà dáadáa. Ẹ ṣeun.
*I'm doing well. Thank you.*

> Notice: you use the respectful **Ẹ** form; she answers with the familiar form back. That's normal — respect flows upward.

## Mini-dialogue 2 — Meeting a friend

**You:** Káàsán o! Báwo ni ń lọ?
*Good afternoon! How's it going?*

**Friend:** Mo wà dáadáa. Ìwọ náà?
*I'm good. And you?*

**You:** Àlàáfíà. Orúkọ mi ni Sarah. Kí ni orúkọ rẹ?
*All is well. My name is Sarah. What's your name?*

## Mini-dialogue 3 — Being welcomed & saying goodbye

**Host:** Ẹ káàbọ̀!
*Welcome!*

**You:** Ẹ ṣeun gan.
*Thank you very much.*

*(later)*

**You:** Ó dàa. Ó di ìgbà mìíràn!
*Goodbye! Until another time!*

**Host:** Ó dàa!
*Goodbye!*

## Practice exercise

1. **Say it out loud** — read each of the 15 greetings aloud three times, exaggerating the tones (high–mid–low) until they feel natural.
2. **Time-of-day drill** — it's 8 AM: which greeting? (Ẹ káàárọ̀ / Káàárọ̀). It's 4 PM with your friend: which? (Káàsán o). It's 8 PM meeting your partner's mother: which? (Ẹ káalẹ́).
3. **Build your intro** — fill in the blanks and say it: "___! Orúkọ mi ni ___. Mo ti ___ wá." (greeting + my name is ___ + I am from ___)
4. **Role-play** — with a partner or in the mirror: run Mini-dialogue 1 twice, switching roles. Second time, swap in the afternoon greetings.

## Cultural note — greeting elders

In Yoruba culture, greeting an elder properly is one of the deepest signs of good upbringing. Young people traditionally **kneel** (kúnlẹ̀ — girls and women) or **prostrate** (dọ̀bálẹ̀ — boys and men, lying flat briefly) when greeting elders, especially in the morning. You are not expected to do this as a learner — but knowing about it shows respect, and elders will be genuinely moved that you understand.

Three things to remember:

1. **Always use the Ẹ form** with anyone older than you or in a position of respect — it's the single fastest way to make a good impression.
2. **Never rush a greeting.** Asking "Báwo ni?" and actually pausing for the answer matters more than perfect tones.
3. **Greet first.** The younger person greets the elder; the visitor greets the host. When in doubt, you greet first.

Master these fifteen phrases and the respect behind them, and you'll already stand out as a thoughtful, welcome guest in any Yoruba-speaking home. Ẹ káàbọ̀ — welcome to the journey!
