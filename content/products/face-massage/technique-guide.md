# Facial Massage Technique Guide

*Your complete reference for lymphatic drainage, gua sha, and jade roller routines. Use this to practice your personalized home routine between sessions.*

> **Note:** This guide is for general wellness and cosmetic purposes only. It is not medical advice. If you have a skin condition or are recovering from a cosmetic procedure, check with your dermatologist before practicing facial massage.

---

## Before You Begin: The Basics

**Clean hands, clean face.** Wash your hands, remove makeup, and cleanse your face.

**Always use slip.** Never massage dry skin — it drags, stretches, and irritates. Apply a few drops of facial oil or serum so your fingers or tool glide smoothly. More is better; reapply if you feel friction.

**Pressure: think light.** Facial massage is not deep-tissue work. If you're pressing hard enough to move the skin around aggressively, you're pressing too hard. Aim for the pressure you'd use to touch a ripe peach without bruising it.

**Time:** 5–10 minutes is plenty for a daily home routine.

## Part 1: Lymphatic Drainage Routine

Lymphatic drainage uses ultra-light, rhythmic strokes to encourage the natural flow of lymph — the fluid that carries waste away from tissues — toward the lymph nodes, where it's processed. Puffiness often comes from fluid that has pooled and stagnated; this routine gets it moving.

**Key principles:**
- Pressure is feather-light — just enough to stretch the skin slightly
- Every stroke moves *toward* the nearest lymph node cluster, then down the neck
- Strokes are slow and rhythmic, never rushed
- Repeat each stroke 5–7 times

### Step-by-step

**Step 1 — Open the drains (neck).**
Place your fingertips flat on the sides of your neck. Sweep downward from just below the ears toward the collarbone with light, slow strokes. This "opens" the pathway so fluid from the face has somewhere to go. Repeat 7 times.

**Step 2 — Collarbone pump.**
With fingertips on your collarbone, make small, gentle pulsing circles — like pressing a tiny doorbell — moving from the center of the collarbone outward toward the shoulders. Repeat 5 times each side. This activates the terminal lymph nodes where everything drains.

**Step 3 — Jawline sweep.**
Place fingertips under your chin at the center. Glide outward along the jawline toward the ears, ending with a light sweep down the sides of the neck to the collarbone. Repeat 5 times each side. Great for jawline definition.

**Step 4 — Cheek sweep.**
Place fingertips at the sides of the nose. Sweep outward across the cheeks toward the ears, then continue the motion down the neck to the collarbone. Use flat fingers, light pressure. Repeat 5 times each side.

**Step 5 — Under-eye sweep.**
Using your ring fingers (naturally the lightest touch), start at the inner corners of the eyes and sweep gently outward along the under-eye area toward the temples. Then sweep down in front of the ears and down the neck. Repeat 5 times. *Never drag or pull the delicate under-eye skin.*

**Step 6 — Forehead sweep.**
Place fingertips at the center of the forehead. Sweep outward toward the temples, then down in front of the ears and down the neck. Repeat 5 times.

**Step 7 — Finish at the neck.**
End where you began: 7 slow downward sweeps on each side of the neck, ears to collarbone. This clears the final pathway.

## Part 2: Gua Sha Routine

Gua sha ("scraping" in Chinese) uses a smooth, flat stone tool to apply medium-light pressure along the face. It helps release muscle tension (especially the jaw and brow), supports circulation, and many people find it deeply relaxing. The technique matters more than the tool — but the tool must be smooth with no chips or sharp edges.

**Key principles:**
- Hold the tool at roughly a **15-degree angle** to the skin — nearly flat, never perpendicular
- **Medium-light pressure:** firmer than lymphatic drainage, but never painful or bruising
- Strokes move **outward and upward** on the face, **downward** on the neck
- **3–5 slow strokes** per area, always in one direction (no back-and-forth sawing)
- Keep the skin oiled; if the tool drags, add more oil

### Step-by-step

**Neck (always first).**
Place the long edge of the stone on the side of your neck. Sweep downward from below the ear to the collarbone. 5 strokes each side. This prepares the drainage pathway.

**Jawline.**
Start at the center of the chin. Place the notched edge of the stone along the jawline and sweep outward toward the earlobe with slow, firm strokes. 5 strokes each side. This is the tension-release zone — many people hold stress here.

**Cheeks.**
Start at the side of the nose. Sweep the flat edge of the stone outward across the cheek toward the ear, then down slightly. Use medium pressure — you may feel mild warmth, which is normal. 5 strokes each side.

**Under-eyes.**
Use the small curved edge. Glide very gently from the inner corner outward toward the temple with light pressure. 3 strokes each side. *This area is delicate — go lighter than everywhere else.*

**Brow and forehead.**
Place the stone above the eyebrow and sweep upward toward the hairline. Work across the brow in sections. Then, for the forehead: start at the center and sweep outward toward the temples. 5 strokes per section. Great for releasing the "thinking frown" tension.

**Finish:** Complete the routine with the neck sweeps from Step 1 of the lymphatic routine — down the neck, 5 strokes each side — to clear everything you mobilized.

### Cleaning your tool
Wash your gua sha stone with warm water and mild soap after each use, rinse well, and dry completely before storing.

## Part 3: Jade Roller Routine — Morning De-Puff Sequence

The jade roller is the gentlest of the three tools and perfect for a quick morning routine. The cool stone calms skin and the rolling motion encourages fluid movement. **Chill your roller in the fridge overnight** for extra de-puffing power.

**Key principles:**
- Roll **outward and upward** on the face, **downward** on the neck
- Light to medium pressure — let the weight of the roller do the work
- **4–6 passes** per area, one direction only
- Use the large end for cheeks/forehead, the small end for under-eyes and around the nose

### Morning de-puff sequence (5 minutes)

1. **Neck:** Roll downward from below the ears to the collarbone, 6 passes each side.
2. **Jawline:** Roll from chin outward to the earlobe, 5 passes each side.
3. **Cheeks:** Roll from nose outward toward the ear, 5 passes each side.
4. **Under-eyes:** With the small end, roll from the inner corner outward to the temple, 4 passes each side. Go slowly.
5. **Eyelids (optional, very light):** One or two gentle passes from inner to outer corner.
6. **Forehead:** Roll from the brow upward to the hairline in sections, 5 passes; then from center outward to the temples, 5 passes.
7. **Finish:** Roll down the neck once more, 5 passes each side.

## Recommended Oils & Serums

Choose based on your skin type. Fragrance-free is best for sensitive skin.

- **Dry skin:** Jojoba oil, squalane oil, rosehip oil — rich, nourishing slip
- **Oily/combination skin:** Squalane (lightweight, non-greasy), grapeseed oil
- **Sensitive skin:** Squalane oil or a simple fragrance-free facial oil — patch-test first
- **Mature skin:** Rosehip oil, argan oil — often paired with a hyaluronic serum underneath

**Avoid:** Coconut oil on the face (can clog pores), essential oils applied undiluted, and any product that stings or burns — stop and rinse if irritation occurs.

## Frequency Guide

| Routine | Recommended frequency |
|---|---|
| Lymphatic drainage | Daily or every other day, 5–10 min |
| Gua sha | 2–3 times per week, 10–15 min |
| Jade roller (morning de-puff) | Daily, 5 min |
| Full combined routine | 1–2 times per week, 20–30 min |

Consistency beats intensity. Five minutes daily does more than one long session a month.

## ⛔ Contraindications — When NOT to Do Facial Massage

Skip facial massage and consult a professional if any of the following apply:

- **Active acne breakouts** — massage can spread bacteria and worsen inflammation
- **Rosacea flare-up** — stimulation can intensify redness and irritation
- **Skin infections** — cold sores, impetigo, fungal infections, or any open sores
- **Recent botox or fillers** — wait at least 2 weeks after botox, 2–4 weeks after fillers (or follow your provider's specific guidance); massage can shift product placement
- **Recent facial procedures** — chemical peels, laser treatments, microneedling: wait until fully healed
- **Sunburn** — wait until the burn has completely healed
- **Undiagnosed lumps, swelling, or rashes** — see a doctor first
- **Blood-thinning medication or easy bruising** — use extra-light pressure or skip gua sha

When in doubt, ask your dermatologist. Facial massage is a wellness practice, not a treatment — and skipping a session when your skin needs rest is always the right call.
