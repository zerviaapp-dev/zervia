# Module 2, Lesson 1 — Greetings & Introductions

Welcome! This is your very first real lesson, and it's the most useful one you'll ever take — because greetings are the first thing you'll say in every single French conversation. By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to greet someone, introduce yourself, be polite, and say goodbye like you mean it.

A quick note on pronunciation: read the **simple phonetics** in brackets out loud as if they were English words. Capital letters mark the stressed syllable (French stress is gentle — it's mostly a slight emphasis on the last syllable). The `~` marks a **nasal vowel** — air flows through your nose, like you're humming the vowel. Try *bon* [boh~]: say "boh" while letting air escape through your nose. It'll feel strange at first. That's normal — you're doing it right.

## 15 essential greetings & introductions

| # | French | Pronunciation | English meaning | Usage notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bonjour | [boh~-ZHOOR] | Hello / Good day | The default greeting. Use it **anywhere, anytime during the day** — shops, streets, phone calls. Always say it before asking anything; skipping it is considered rude. |
| 2 | Bonsoir | [boh~-SWAH] | Good evening | Used from roughly 6 PM onward instead of *Bonjour*. |
| 3 | Salut | [sah-LU] | Hi / Bye (informal) | For friends, peers, and young people. **Never** with strangers, bosses, or elders — it's *tu*-territory only. |
| 4 | Bonne journée | [bun zhoor-NAY] | Have a good day | Said when **leaving** (shopkeepers say it as you walk out). Not a greeting — a farewell. |
| 5 | Bonne soirée | [bun swah-RAY] | Have a good evening | Same idea as above, for the evening. |
| 6 | Je m'appelle… | [zhuh mah-PELL] | My name is… | Literally "I call myself…". The standard self-introduction: *Je m'appelle Marie.* |
| 7 | Moi, c'est… | [mwah say] | I'm… (casual) | Friendly, informal alternative: *Moi, c'est David.* Fine with peers; too casual for formal settings. |
| 8 | Enchanté / Enchantée | [ah~-shah~-TAY] | Nice to meet you | Men say *enchanté*, women say *enchantée* (pronounced the same). Use after introductions. |
| 9 | Comment allez-vous ? | [kuh-mah~-tah-lay VOO] | How are you? (formal) | Uses **vous** — for strangers, elders, professionals. The safe default. |
| 10 | Comment ça va ? | [kuh-mah~ sah VAH] | How's it going? (informal) | Uses **tu** — friends, family, people your age in casual settings. |
| 11 | Ça va bien, merci. | [sah vah byeh~ mair-SEE] | I'm well, thanks. | The standard polite answer. You can also ask back: *Et vous ?* / *Et toi ?* |
| 12 | Merci | [mair-SEE] | Thank you | Say it often — the French use it constantly, and so should you. |
| 13 | S'il vous plaît | [seel voo PLAY] | Please (formal) | Literally "if it pleases you." Use with **vous** — shops, restaurants, strangers. |
| 14 | S'il te plaît | [seel tuh PLAY] | Please (informal) | The **tu** version — friends and family only. |
| 15 | Au revoir | [oh ruh-VWAHR] | Goodbye | The standard, all-purpose goodbye. When in doubt, this one. |

**Two bonus goodbyes:** *À bientôt* [ah byeh~-TOH] ("see you soon") and *À demain* [ah duh-MEH~] ("see you tomorrow") — warmer than *au revoir* when you'll actually see the person again.

## Mini-dialogue 1 — Meeting a stranger (formal, vous)

**Marie:** Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Marie.
*(Hello! My name is Marie.)*

**Paul:** Bonjour, Marie. Je m'appelle Paul. Enchanté.
*(Hello, Marie. My name is Paul. Nice to meet you.)*

**Marie:** Enchantée ! Comment allez-vous ?
*(Nice to meet you! How are you?)*

**Paul:** Ça va bien, merci. Et vous ?
*(I'm well, thanks. And you?)*

**Marie:** Très bien, merci !
*(Very well, thanks!)*

> Notice: two strangers = **vous** all the way. This is the safe, respectful default.

## Mini-dialogue 2 — Meeting a friend of a friend (informal, tu)

**Léa:** Salut ! Moi, c'est Léa.
*(Hi! I'm Léa.)*

**Hugo:** Salut, Léa ! Moi, c'est Hugo. Comment ça va ?
*(Hi, Léa! I'm Hugo. How's it going?)*

**Léa:** Ça va bien, et toi ?
*(Good, and you?)*

**Hugo:** Super, merci !
*(Great, thanks!)*

> Same age, casual setting = **tu**. *Salut* and *ça va* mark the informal register.

## Mini-dialogue 3 — At a shop (politeness in action)

**Client:** Bonjour !
*(Hello!)*

**Vendeuse:** Bonjour ! Je peux vous aider ?
*(Hello! Can I help you?)*

**Client:** Oui, s'il vous plaît. Je cherche un cadeau.
*(Yes, please. I'm looking for a gift.)*

**Vendeuse:** Bien sûr. *(Of course.)*

**Client:** Merci beaucoup ! Au revoir, bonne journée !
*(Thank you very much! Goodbye, have a good day!)*

**Vendeuse:** Au revoir, bonne journée !
*(Goodbye, have a good day!)*

> *Bonjour* opens, *s'il vous plaît* softens the request, *bonne journée* closes. This little ritual is the heartbeat of French politeness.

## Practice exercise — say it out loud

Do these out loud, not in your head. French lives in the mouth.

1. **Introduce yourself twice:** once formally (*Bonjour, je m'appelle… Enchanté(e).*) and once casually (*Salut ! Moi, c'est…*).
2. **Greet an imaginary shopkeeper:** walk through Dialogue 3, playing both parts. Don't forget *bonne journée* on the way out!
3. **Answer the question:** someone asks *Comment allez-vous ?* — reply *Ça va bien, merci. Et vous ?* Then try the informal version: *Comment ça va ?* → *Ça va bien, et toi ?*
4. **Nasal vowel drill:** say *bon, mon, non, maison* — hold your nose lightly while saying them. If the sound changes, you're nasalizing correctly!

## Cultural note — La bise: the cheek-kiss greeting

In much of France, friends and acquaintances greet with **la bise** — a light kiss (really more of a cheek touch with a kissing sound) on each cheek. A few things to know:

- **How many?** It varies by region — two is standard in Paris, but three or even four happen in parts of the south. When in doubt, follow the other person's lead.
- **Who?** Friends, family, friendly colleagues. **Not** strangers, and **not** in formal business first meetings (a handshake is the safe default there).
- **The technique:** lean in, turn your head slightly to the right, touch right cheeks, make a small kissing sound, then switch. Keep it light — no actual lip-to-cheek contact needed.
- **Men** typically shake hands with each other; la bise is common between women, and between men and women who know each other.
- **The golden rule:** if someone moves in for la bise, go with it gracefully. Hesitating mid-lean is how accidental headbutts happen — every French person has a story.

As a learner, nobody expects you to initiate la bise. A warm *Bonjour* with a smile is always correct. The kisses come with the friendships.
