# Learn Hausa — From Zero to Confident Conversations
### A beginner-to-conversational ecourse taught in English

Hausa is spoken by over 60 million people across northern Nigeria, Niger, Ghana, Cameroon, and beyond — it's the lingua franca of West African trade. This course takes you from your very first word to holding real, warm, everyday conversations. Every lesson is practical: the phrases people actually say in markets, homes, and on the street — plus the cultural notes that keep you from accidentally stepping on a toe.

---

## Module 1 — Sound It Out: Pronunciation & the Boko Script
Get the sounds right first. Hausa is written in the Latin alphabet (called *Boko*), so there's no new script to learn — but it has a few special sounds worth practicing early.

1. **Meet the Boko alphabet** — The 28 letters of written Hausa, which English letters to ignore, and why reading Hausa is easier than it looks.
2. **ƙ ɗ ɓ ƴ: the famous "hooked" sounds** — Step-by-step mouth guide for Hausa's four implosive/ejective consonants (with the English sound they're closest to).
3. **The glottal stop (') and long vowels** — Hearing and saying the little catch in the throat, plus long vs. short vowels that change meaning.
4. **c, ts, and the trilled r** — Why Hausa *c* sounds like "ch," how to pop a *ts*, and rolling your r the West African way.
5. **Tones: high, low, and falling** — Hausa is tonal — the same word can mean different things at different pitches. How to hear and copy the melody of a sentence.
6. **Sound drills with real words** — A guided practice set of everyday words that trains every special sound in this module.

## Module 2 — Greetings & Introductions
Hausa greetings are famously elaborate — and getting them right opens every door. This module makes you a confident greeter from day one.

1. **Sannu! The all-purpose hello** — Your first Hausa word, and the response *Lafiya lau* (fine, peaceful).
2. **The greeting rounds** — Why one hello is never enough: how Hausa people trade greetings back and forth, and how to keep up.
3. **Time-of-day greetings** — *Ina kwana* (good morning), *Ina yini* (good afternoon), *Ina wuni* (good evening), and the *Barka da…* family.
4. **Introducing yourself** — *Sunanka?* (what's your name?), *Sunana…* (my name is…), *Daga ina kake?* (where are you from?).
5. **Please, thank you, excuse me** — *Don Allah* (please), *Na gode* (thank you), *Madalla!* (bravo/well done), *Yi hakuri* (excuse me/forgive me).
6. **Saying goodbye warmly** — *Sai an jima* (see you later), *Sai da safe* (see you in the morning), and parting like a friend, not a tourist.
7. **Respectful forms for elders & strangers** — *Ranka ya daɗe* / *Ranki ya daɗe* (may your life be long), and why elders always get extra greeting rounds.

## Module 3 — Numbers, Time & Dates
Count, tell time, and handle dates — the building blocks of shopping, travel, and appointments.

1. **Numbers 1–10** — *ɗaya, biyu, uku…* with pronunciation practice (ɗ shows up early!).
2. **Numbers 11–100 and beyond** — Building bigger numbers the Hausa way, plus thousands and millions.
3. **Telling time** — *Karfe nawa?* (what time is it?), hours, half-hours, and "morning/noon/evening" time blocks.
4. **Days, weeks & months** — Days of the week (with Friday, *Juma'a*, holding special status), and the Islamic and Gregorian months.
5. **"When?" and scheduling** — *Yaushe?* (when?), today/tomorrow/yesterday, morning/afternoon/evening, and making plans.

## Module 4 — Food & Dining
Eat like a local: name dishes, order food, and survive a Nigerian restaurant or family invitation with grace.

1. **Staple dishes you must know** — *tuwo*, *miyan kuka*, *suya*, *kosai*, *fura da nono*, and what to expect when each arrives.
2. **Ordering at a restaurant or roadside spot** — *Ina so…* (I want…), asking what's available, and paying the bill.
3. **Drinks & snacks** — *Kunu*, *zobo*, bottled water (*ruwa*), and street snacks like *dankwali* and roasted corn.
4. **"Is it spicy?" and dietary needs** — *Yana da yaji?* (is it peppery?), explaining allergies, and polite ways to decline food.
5. **Eating as a guest** — Right-hand rule, washing hands, *Bismillah* before eating, and the compliments that make your host's day.

## Module 5 — Shopping, Markets & Money
Haggle at the market, ask prices, and handle money with the confidence of a regular.

1. **Money basics** — The naira and kobo, saying amounts, and counting cash out loud.
2. **Asking the price** — *Nawa ne?* (how much is it?), *Nawa kudin…?*, and understanding the answer in fast market Hausa.
3. **Bargaining like a local** — *Ka rage mini* (reduce it for me), the polite back-and-forth, and when to walk away smiling.
4. **Market vocabulary** — Cloth, foodstuffs, quantities (*kwano*, *mudu* — traditional measures), and sizes.
5. **"Do you have…?" and finding things** — *Kuna da…?* (do you have…?), asking for something specific, and navigating a big market.
6. **Paying & change** — *Bani canji* (give me change), checking your change, and transfer/payment phrases for modern shops.

## Module 6 — Travel & Directions
Get around town: taxis, directions, and the phrases that keep you oriented.

1. **Getting a taxi or keke** — *Nawa zuwa…?* (how much to…?), negotiating the fare before you get in, and *Tsaya!* (stop!).
2. **Asking for directions** — *Ina…?* (where is…?), understanding "left/right/straight" and landmark-based directions.
3. **At the motor park & on the road** — Booking a seat, asking departure times, and roadside phrases for long journeys.
4. **Hotels & lodging** — *Ina so ɗaki* (I want a room), asking about price, water, and light (electricity).
5. **"I'm lost" and getting help** — The calm phrases that get a stranger to walk you to your destination.

## Module 7 — Family, Friends & Social Life
Talk about the people who matter, make friends, and join in the social rhythms of Hausa life.

1. **Family vocabulary** — *uba* (father), *uwa* (mother), *yaya* (older sibling), *ƙane* (younger brother), *'yar'uwa* (sister), and the extended family that matters so much in Hausa culture.
2. **"Do you have children?"** — *Kana da yara?* and the warm small talk that follows a greeting round.
3. **Making friends** — *Mu zama abokai* (let's be friends), exchanging numbers, and casual hangout phrases.
4. **Weddings, naming ceremonies & celebrations** — *Barka da…* greetings for every occasion, and what to say (and wear) when invited.
5. **Phone & messaging phrases** — *Wayata?* calls, WhatsApp small talk, and texting in Hausa.

## Module 8 — Work & Business Phrases
For the professional visitor: introductions, meetings, and the polite language of doing business.

1. **Introducing yourself professionally** — *Ni… ne daga…* (I am… from…), your role, and your company.
2. **Meeting & appointment language** — *Muna da taro* (we have a meeting), rescheduling, and "I'll be there at…".
3. **Polite requests & instructions** — Softening requests with *Don Allah*, giving clear instructions, and confirming understanding (*Ka/Kin gane?* — do you understand?).
4. **Prices, quantities & agreements** — Business numbers, delivery dates, and confirming a deal verbally.
5. **Small talk before business** — The greeting rounds that precede every meeting, and why rushing them costs you goodwill.

## Module 9 — Health, Emergencies & Everyday Help
The phrases you hope you'll never need — and the everyday ones you'll use constantly.

1. **"I need help"** — *Ina bukatar taimako*, getting attention, and staying calm in an emergency.
2. **At the clinic or pharmacy** — *Ina ciwo* (I am sick/in pain), describing symptoms, and asking for medicine.
3. **Body parts & common ailments** — Head, stomach, fever (*zazzabi*), and the words that get you the right treatment.
4. **Everyday services** — *Ina…?* for the bank, the tailor, the barber, charging your phone, and buying data.
5. **Weather & seasons** — *Rani* (dry season), *damina* (rainy season), *zafi* (heat), and the small talk everyone shares.

## Module 10 — Culture, Etiquette & Real Conversation Practice
Put it all together: the customs behind the words, and guided practice that turns phrases into real conversation.

1. **Islamic customs & daily rhythms** — Prayer times, Ramadan basics for visitors, *Barka da Juma'a* (blessed Friday), and *In sha Allah* (God willing) in everyday speech.
2. **Greeting rituals, deepened** — When to stand, when to sit, the right hand, and greetings as a sign of respect — not just politeness.
3. **Gender-appropriate greetings** — Verbal greetings vs. handshakes, following the other person's lead, and respectful address for men and women.
4. **Do's and don'ts cheat sheet** — Shoes, the left hand, pointing, photographing people, and accepting gifts.
5. **Proverbs & expressions that make you sound local** — *Hankuri da yawa* (much patience), *Gaskiya!* (truly!), and sayings that earn smiles.
6. **Guided conversation practice: market day** — A full walkthrough dialogue combining greetings, numbers, and bargaining.
7. **Guided conversation practice: meeting a family** — Introductions, small talk, and parting — start to finish.
8. **Your 30-day conversation plan** — A realistic daily practice routine (15–20 minutes a day) to keep your Hausa growing after the course ends.
