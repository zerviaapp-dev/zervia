# Lesson 01 — Greetings & Introductions
*Module 2 · The phrases you'll use every single day*

Welcome! This lesson is useful all by itself: learn these 12 phrases and you can greet anyone, introduce yourself, and say goodbye — politely, anywhere in the Arab world.

> **How to read this lesson:** Every phrase shows the **Arabic script**, then **transliteration** (how it sounds in Latin letters), then the **English meaning**. Read the script out loud even if it's new to you — that's how it becomes yours.

---

## The Essential 12

| # | Arabic | Transliteration | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | السلام عليكم | as-salāmu ʿalaykum | Peace be upon you (the universal greeting) |
| 2 | وعليكم السلام | wa ʿalaykum as-salām | And upon you be peace (the reply) |
| 3 | مرحباً | marḥaban | Hello (warm, general) |
| 4 | أهلاً وسهلاً | ahlan wa sahlan | Welcome! (literally "you've come to family and ease") |
| 5 | صباح الخير | ṣabāḥ al-khayr | Good morning |
| 6 | مساء الخير | masāʾ al-khayr | Good evening |
| 7 | كيف حالك؟ | kayfa ḥāluk? | How are you? (to a man) |
| 8 | كيف حالِك؟ | kayfa ḥālik? | How are you? (to a woman) |
| 9 | اسمي… | ismī… | My name is… |
| 10 | شكراً | shukran | Thank you |

## Pronunciation Guide

Arabic has a few sounds English doesn't. Here are the ones in this lesson:

- **ʿ (as in ʿalaykum)** — ع (*ʿayn*): a voiced sound from deep in the throat. Think of a gentle squeeze at the back of your throat, like the start of a soft gargle — but voiced, almost like a vowel. It feels strange at first; everyone struggles, and Arabs love you for trying.
- **ḥ (as in ḥāluk, marḥaban)** — ح (*ḥāʾ*): a breathy *h* from the throat, like fogging a mirror but stronger. Whisper "haaa" and tighten slightly — that's it.
- **kh (as in khayr)** — خ: the throaty "ch" of Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*.
- **ṣ (as in ṣabāḥ)** — ص: like *s*, but "heavy" — lower your tongue slightly and round your lips a touch.
- **q** — ق: a *k* made at the very back of the throat, near the uvula.

**Don't aim for perfection — aim for effort.** Approximate these sounds confidently and you'll be understood everywhere. The exact shapes come with practice.

## Usage Notes: Formal vs. Informal

- **As-salāmu ʿalaykum / wa ʿalaykum as-salām** is the all-purpose greeting — formal, informal, strangers, friends, any time of day. If someone greets you this way, answering *wa ʿalaykum as-salām* is both polite and expected. You can shorten it among friends to **salām** (سلام).
- **Marḥaban** and **ahlan wa sahlan** are warm and friendly; *ahlan wa sahlan* is what a host says to make you feel truly welcome. Reply with **ahlan bīk** (to a man) / **ahlan bīki** (to a woman) — "welcome to you too."
- **Min faḍlik** is literally "from your kindness" — use it freely; it softens every request. (To a woman: **min faḍlik** becomes **min faḍliki** — you'll learn these endings as you go; *min faḍlik* is fine everywhere for now.)
- **Maʿa as-salāma** is the standard goodbye. Among friends you'll also hear **ilā al-liqāʾ** (إلى اللقاء — "until we meet").

**Dialect notes:** In Egypt, you'll hear **izayyak** (إزيك) for "how are you?" and **ahlan** alone as "hi." In the Levant (Jordan, Lebanon, Syria), **kīfak** (كيفك) is the everyday "how are you?" In the Gulf, **shlōnak** (شلونك) does the same job. MSA *kayfa ḥāluk* is understood everywhere — it's your safe default.

## 3 Mini-Dialogues

**1 · Meeting a stranger**

> **Layla:** السلام عليكم! *(as-salāmu ʿalaykum!)* — Peace be upon you!
> **Omar:** وعليكم السلام. أهلاً وسهلاً! *(wa ʿalaykum as-salām. Ahlan wa sahlan!)* — And upon you be peace. Welcome!
> **Layla:** اسمي ليلى. *(ismī Layla.)* — My name is Layla.
> **Omar:** اسمي عمر. كيف حالك؟ *(ismī Omar. Kayfa ḥāluk?)* — My name is Omar. How are you?
> **Layla:** بخير، شكراً. *(bikhayr, shukran.)* — Well, thank you.

*(بخير bikhayr = "well/fine" — your new favorite answer.)*

**2 · Morning at a café**

> **You:** صباح الخير! *(ṣabāḥ al-khayr!)* — Good morning!
> **Barista:** صباح النور! *(ṣabāḥ an-nūr!)* — Morning of light! *(the lovely standard reply)*
> **You:** من فضلك، قهوة. *(min faḍlik, qahwa.)* — A coffee, please.
> **Barista:** تفضل. *(tafaḍḍal.)* — Here you go. *(said to a man; to a woman: tafaḍḍalī)*
> **You:** شكراً! مع السلامة. *(shukran! Maʿa as-salāma.)* — Thank you! Goodbye.

**3 · Saying goodbye to a new friend**

> **You:** سعدت بلقائك! *(saʿidtu biliqāʾik!)* — Nice to meet you! *(lit. "I was happy to meet you")*
> **Friend:** وأنا أيضاً! *(wa anā ayḍan!)* — Me too!
> **You:** مع السلامة! *(maʿa as-salāma!)* — Goodbye!
> **Friend:** إلى اللقاء! *(ilā al-liqāʾ!)* — Until we meet!

## Cultural Note: Hospitality & the Right Hand 🤝

Arab hospitality is legendary — and greetings are its front door. Expect handshakes, a hand over the heart (a warm, respectful gesture), and among friends, cheek kisses. Take your time with greetings; rushing past "how are you?" to business feels rude. Ask about health, family, and the day — the small talk *is* the relationship.

**The right hand rule:** Always greet, give, receive, and eat with your **right hand**. The left hand is considered unclean, and offering something with it — even by accident — can offend. If you're left-handed, a smile and a quick switch to the right hand goes a long way.

If you're invited for tea or coffee, accept — refusing hospitality can feel like refusing friendship. You'll rarely regret saying yes.
