# Lesson 1 — Greetings & Introductions

Welcome! By the end of this lesson you'll be able to greet anyone in Tagalog, introduce yourself, say goodbye warmly — and understand the politeness system that makes Filipinos light up when foreigners get it right. Take it slow, say everything out loud, and don't worry about being perfect. Effort is the whole point.

---

## Part 1 — Essential Greetings & Introductions

Pronunciation guide: Tagalog vowels always sound the same — **a** as in "father," **e** as in "bed," **i** as in "machine," **o** as in "go," **u** as in "flute." Stress the CAPITALIZED syllable.

| # | Tagalog | Pronunciation | English meaning | Usage notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kumusta?** | koo-moos-TAH | How are you? / Hello! | The all-purpose greeting. From Spanish "¿cómo está?" Answer: "Mabuti" (I'm fine) or "Mabuti naman" (I'm fine, and you?). |
| 2 | **Kumusta ka?** | koo-moos-TAH kah | How are you? (informal) | For friends and people your age. Adding "ka" (you) makes it casual and warm. |
| 3 | **Kumusta po kayo?** | koo-moos-TAH poh kah-YOH | How are you? (formal/respectful) | For elders, bosses, strangers. "Po" = respect particle; "kayo" = formal "you." Use this and watch faces soften. |
| 4 | **Magandang umaga** | mah-gahn-DAHNG oo-MAH-gah | Good morning | Until ~11 AM. "Maganda" (beautiful) + "umaga" (morning) = "beautiful morning." |
| 5 | **Magandang hapon** | mah-gahn-DAHNG HAH-pon | Good afternoon | ~12 PM to ~6 PM. |
| 6 | **Magandang gabi** | mah-gahn-DAHNG gah-BEE | Good evening | After ~6 PM. Also used as "good night" when parting in the evening. |
| 7 | **Ako si…** | ah-KOH see | I am… / My name is… | "Ako si Maria" = "I'm Maria." The standard self-introduction. |
| 8 | **Anong pangalan mo?** | ah-NOHNG pah-NGAH-lahn moh | What is your name? (informal) | For peers. With elders/strangers: "Ano pong pangalan ninyo?" |
| 9 | **Taga-saan ka?** | tah-gah sah-AHN kah | Where are you from? | One of the first 3 questions Filipinos always ask. Answer: "Taga-Calgary ako" (I'm from Calgary). |
| 10 | **Ikinagagalak kitang makilala** | ee-kee-nah-gah-GAH-lahk kee-TAHNG mah-kee-LAH-lah | Pleased to meet you | Formal and a bit long — perfectly fine to shorten to "Nice to meet you!" in English with a smile, then continue in Tagalog. |
| 11 | **Salamat** | sah-LAH-maht | Thank you | Works everywhere. |
| 12 | **Salamat po** | sah-LAH-maht poh | Thank you (respectful) | Add "po" for elders, vendors, anyone you want to honor. Costs nothing, means everything. |
| 13 | **Paalam** | pah-AH-lahm | Goodbye (formal) | Literally "farewell." A bit formal — used when someone is leaving for a long time. |
| 14 | **Sige** | SEE-geh | Okay / Alright / Bye (casual) | The everyday goodbye among friends: "Sige, ingat!" = "Alright, take care!" |
| 15 | **Ingat** | ee-NGAHT | Take care | Said to everyone, always. "Ingat ka" (take care, informal) / "Ingat po kayo" (respectful). Filipinos say this the way English speakers say "bye." |

### The "po / opo" system — read this twice

- **Po** is a respect particle you tuck into sentences: "Salamat po," "Magandang umaga po." It signals respect to elders, strangers, and anyone in authority. There is no English equivalent — think of it as built-in politeness.
- **Opo** = respectful "yes." **Hindi po** = respectful "no."
- Informal yes/no: **oo** (oh-OH) / **hindi** (heen-DEE).
- Rule of thumb: anyone clearly older than you, anyone serving you, anyone you just met → use **po**. Friends your age → skip it. When in doubt, use po — nobody is ever offended by extra respect.

## Part 2 — Mini-Dialogues

### Dialogue 1 — Meeting a friend of a friend
- **Liza:** Kumusta? Ako si Liza. *(koo-moos-TAH? ah-KOH see LEE-zah)* — Hi! I'm Liza.
- **You:** Kumusta! Ako si Alex. *(koo-moos-TAH! ah-KOH see Alex)* — Hi! I'm Alex.
- **Liza:** Taga-saan ka? *(tah-gah sah-AHN kah)* — Where are you from?
- **You:** Taga-Edmonton ako. Ikaw? *(tah-gah edmonton ah-KOH. ee-KAHW?)* — I'm from Edmonton. You?
- **Liza:** Taga-Maynila ako. *(tah-gah mahy-NEE-lah ah-KOH)* — I'm from Manila.

### Dialogue 2 — Greeting an elder respectfully
- **You:** Magandang umaga po. Kumusta po kayo? *(mah-gahn-DAHNG oo-MAH-gah poh. koo-moos-TAH poh kah-YOH)* — Good morning. How are you?
- **Lola:** Mabuti naman. Ikaw, iha? *(mah-BOO-tee nah-MAHN. ee-KAHW, EE-hah?)* — I'm well. And you, dear?
- **You:** Mabuti rin po. Salamat po. *(mah-BOO-tee reen poh. sah-LAH-maht poh)* — I'm well too. Thank you.

### Dialogue 3 — Saying goodbye after dinner
- **Host:** Salamat sa pagpunta! *(sah-LAH-maht sah pahg-poon-TAH!)* — Thanks for coming!
- **You:** Salamat din po sa masarap na hapunan! *(sah-LAH-maht deen poh sah mah-sah-RAHP nah hah-poo-NAHN!)* — Thank you too for the delicious dinner!
- **Host:** Sige, ingat kayo! *(SEE-geh, ee-NGAHT kah-YOH!)* — Alright, take care!
- **You:** Ingat din po! Paalam! *(ee-NGAHT deen poh! pah-AH-lahm!)* — Take care too! Goodbye!

## Part 3 — Practice Exercise

Do this out loud — whispering counts, but out loud is better.

1. **Greet the day:** Look at the clock. Say the correct "Magandang ___" for this time of day, 3 times.
2. **Introduce yourself:** Say "Kumusta? Ako si [your name]. Taga-[your city] ako." Repeat until it flows.
3. **The respect switch:** Say each pair, feeling the difference — "Salamat" → "Salamat po" … "Kumusta ka?" → "Kumusta po kayo?" … "Ingat ka" → "Ingat po kayo."
4. **Role-play:** Read Dialogue 2 playing BOTH parts. Then read Dialogue 1. Notice which one uses "po" and which doesn't — that's the whole system.
5. **Real-world mission:** This week, greet one Filipino you know with "Kumusta?" instead of "Hi." Report back how they reacted. (Spoiler: they'll grin.)

## Cultural Note 🇵🇭

In the Philippines, greetings are relationship work, not small talk. Asking "Kumusta?" and actually waiting for the answer — then asking about family — is how trust is built. And the "po/opo" system isn't optional decoration: skipping it with an elder can feel as jarring as calling your boss by their first name on day one in some workplaces. The beautiful part? Filipinos don't expect foreigners to get it right — so when you do, even imperfectly, the warmth you get back is ten times the effort. Start with "po," smile big, and you're already family.
