# Home Plumbing Basics — Full Curriculum

*4 modules • self-paced • lifetime access. All lessons include a "when to call a pro" checkpoint.*

> **Disclaimer:** This course is for educational purposes only. Always follow your local plumbing codes (Canada), and note that some jobs legally require a licensed plumber or a permit — gas line work, main line connections, and certain installations. When in doubt, call a professional.

---

## Module 1: Plumbing Basics & Safety ✅ *(written — see module-1-basics.md)*

The foundation everything else stands on: how water moves through your home, where to stop it in an emergency, and the safety mindset that keeps small jobs small.

1. **How Residential Plumbing Works: Two Systems, One House** — the pressurized supply system that brings clean water in, and the gravity-fed DWV (drain-waste-vent) system that takes wastewater out. How they work together, and why they're completely separate.
2. **Find Your Main Water Shut-Off (Do This First)** — where main shut-offs live in Canadian homes (basement, utility room, crawl space), how to operate gate vs. ball valves, and the "test it on a calm Saturday" rule. Every home, every rental — know this before anything else.
3. **Fixture Shut-Offs: Your First Line of Defense** — the little valves under sinks and behind toilets: what they look like, how they fail when they haven't been touched in years, and how to exercise them safely.
4. **Water Pressure Basics** — what's normal (40–60 psi), how to read a pressure gauge, and why high pressure quietly destroys fixtures, valves, and water heaters.
5. **Pipe Materials You'll Encounter** — copper, PEX, PVC/ABS, and older galvanized steel: how to identify each, where each is used, and which ones a beginner should never try to cut or join.

## Module 2: Tools of the Trade

The small kit that handles 90% of beginner repairs — what each tool does, what to buy, and what to skip.

1. **Plungers: Cup vs. Flange (Yes, It Matters)** — the flat cup plunger is for sinks and tubs; the flange plunger with the extended rubber lip is for toilets. Using the wrong one is why plunging "never works" for so many people.
2. **Drain Augers: Your Clog's Worst Enemy** — hand augers (drain snakes) for sinks and tubs, closet augers for toilets: how they work, how to feed the cable without scratching fixtures, and when to stop.
3. **Wrenches: Adjustable, Basin, and Channel-Lock** — which wrench for which job, how to protect chrome finishes with a cloth, and the two-wrench rule for loosening fittings without twisting pipes.
4. **Thread Tapes and Sealants** — PTFE (Teflon) tape, pipe dope, and plumber's putty: what each one seals, where each one goes, and the "wrap it the right direction" rule.
5. **The Rest of the Kit: Buckets, Towels, and Headlamps** — the unglamorous supplies that turn a disaster into a minor inconvenience, plus what NOT to buy (chemical drain cleaners, for starters).

## Module 3: Fixing Leaks & Drips

The repairs that pay for the course: drip by drip, leak by leak, the easy wins.

1. **Faucet Cartridge Replacement, Step by Step** — identifying your faucet type (cartridge, compression, ball, ceramic disc), shutting off and verifying, removing the handle and cartridge, matching the replacement at the store, and reassembling without leaks.
2. **Supply Line Leaks: Spotting, Tightening, Replacing** — the braided lines under your sinks and behind your toilet: checking for bulges and corrosion, the quarter-turn rule for tightening, and when to replace instead of tighten.
3. **Toilet Tank Fixes: Flapper and Fill Valve** — the dye test (food colouring in the tank reveals a leaking flapper in minutes), replacing a flapper, adjusting or replacing a fill valve, and setting the correct water level.
4. **Slow Leaks: The Silent Budget Killers** — where slow leaks hide (under sinks, behind toilets, at the water heater base), the paper-towel test, and why a $5 part today beats an insurance claim next year.
5. **Pressure-Test Your Work** — turning the water back on slowly, checking every connection you touched (and the ones nearby), and the 24-hour watch rule before calling a repair "done."

---

## Module 4: Unclogging Drains & Toilet Repairs

Clogs and toilet trouble — the jobs everyone dreads and most people can actually handle.

1. **Plunger Technique: The Right Way** — most people plunge wrong: the seal, the water level, the push-pull rhythm, and how many strokes before you change tactics. Sink plunging (cover the overflow!) vs. toilet plunging.
2. **Using a Hand Drain Auger Safely** — feeding the cable, feeling for the clog vs. a bend in the pipe, cranking without forcing, and pulling back slowly. What to do when the auger grabs something.
3. **Toilet Flapper and Fill Valve Replacement** — the two parts that cause 90% of toilet problems: diagnosis (running toilet? weak flush? phantom refills?), then replacement with the water off and the tank drained.
4. **Wax Ring Basics: What It Is and Why It's Usually a Pro Job** — the wax seal between toilet and flange: signs it's failing (water at the base, sewer smell, a rocking toilet), and why lifting a toilet is the line most beginners shouldn't cross — with the honest exceptions.
5. **The Complete "When to Call a Plumber" Framework** — the final decision tree: main line issues, sewer backups, gas lines (never), water heater internals, anything behind a wall you're not sure about, and the golden rule — if water is flowing where it shouldn't and you can't stop it, the pro call is already overdue.
